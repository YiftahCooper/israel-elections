# K9 candidate capacity: successful hypothesis, explicit legal uncertainty

The owner authorized testing and, on exact reproduction, using this hypothesis:
cap each list at its known full candidate count, then continue the section 81(d)
highest-quotient remainder procedure, retaining section 82 pair units. Cap whole-quota
allocations as well as remainder allocations; sum partner capacities nationally and
apply individual capacities during internal re-division. No vote is changed.

`RuleSet.seatsCappedAtCandidates` is optional and off when absent/false. Only the
K9 preset enables it. `ListInput.candidateCount` supplies a known complete count;
unknown counts are unbounded, never equated to the length of a partial transcription.
Scenario conversion therefore supplies counts only for complete available rosters.
The complete one-candidate פש roster is sufficient to establish the baseline K9 cap;
other winning lists have reviewed prefixes long enough to hold their published seats.
This does **not** certify capacity-sensitive counterfactuals with unknown full rosters.
Scenario export/import, historical validation and departure detection carry the flag.
No `src/app/` files were changed. A future Rules panel option should say “Cap seats at
known candidate counts”, explain the inference for 1977, and disclose unbounded unknown
rosters; it should be editable only in experimental mode like the other rules.

Using all six published agreements, the optional procedure reproduces **every list**:
פש **2 → 1**, ש **1 → 2**; all others unchanged, including NRP **12**. Both tables are
retained in 0075 and tested. The uncapped calculation remains available with the option
off; other historical presets retain it off. Independent reference tests cover initial
caps, exhausted capacity and pair-unit/internal division. Full test-suite evidence is
recorded in the implementation report.

## Provenance and search limits

Verified original authority remains SH 556 (1969), pp.113/116 (§§67,81–84), and SH 692
p.108 (1973 Amendment **4**, replacing §81(d)); both are retained and hashed in SOURCES.
Section 83 identifies the leading names as members but supplies no explicit redistribution
algorithm. YP 2326 pp.1494–1496 publishes the votes, seats and elected names, with no
stated explanation of the unfillable seat. YP 2319 p.1330 prints the sole פש candidate,
**שרון סמואל**, also printed as elected in YP 2326 p.1496.

Further Hebrew searches of government/Knesset material, Flatto-Sharon judgments and
CBS leads did **not locate the statutory text or a contemporaneous CEC decision** for
this specific redistribution. The indexed CEC discussion of criminal appeal 71/83 is
about election bribery, not this allocation; direct retrieval returned 403. The official
“1977 by polling station” download inspected here starts with regional station printouts,
not a methodological chapter; only its first six PDF pages were inspected. It is not a
verified complete CBS methodological volume. Nearby YP 2327–2334 covers were checked
for an elected-name correction; none was identified. This is bounded search, not proof
that the authority/correction does not exist. Exact queries, URLs and inspection scopes
are retained in `data/raw/k9/search.reviewed.json`, registered in SOURCES.

**The procedure is inferred from the officially published result because the statutory
text was not located.** Reproduction supports this implementation, not a claim of unique
historical legal authority. K9 nevertheless stays **unavailable**: YP 2326 omits the Ratz
(רצ) elected-member paragraph; its seat table gives one seat but the names section lists
only 119 people. Both the government and olaw copies have that omission. All winning
candidate prefixes (120 ranks) are transcribed, but only **119/120 elected names** can be
cross-checked against that notice. The missing cross-check is not silently replaced by
knowledge that Shulamit Aloni served in the Knesset.
