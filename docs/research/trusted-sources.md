## Newspapers: identify the era and the desk

These working orientations depend on era and ownership. Use attributable news, not editorials or opinion.

| Paper and useful era | Orientation to record with a citation |
|---|---|
| **Davar, 1925–1996** | Histadrut daily; Mapai/Labor establishment. [NLI title record](https://www.nli.org.il/he/newspapers/dav). |
| **Herut, 1948–1965** | Herut party organ. [NLI history](https://www.nli.org.il/he/newspapers/hrt/1949/02). |
| **Al HaMishmar, 1943–1995** | Hashomer Hatzair/Mapam paper: socialist Zionist left. [NLI title history](https://www.nli.org.il/he/newspapers/ahr), available here through search text. |
| **HaTzofe, 1937–2008** | Mizrahi, then NRP organ; national-religious after its 2003 sale. [NLI history](https://www.nli.org.il/he/newspapers/hzh/1955/08). |
| **Maariv, from 1948** | Commercial/independent; early right-Zionist tradition, critical of Mapai. [NLI history](https://www.nli.org.il/he/newspapers/mar/2020/08). |
| **Yedioth Ahronoth, from 1939** | Commercial mass-circulation, varied voices; no permanent left/right label. Ask a librarian about issue access. |
| **Haaretz, from 1919** | Independent/liberal; modern editorial position generally Zionist left. [NLI account](https://blog.nli.org.il/tag/הארץ/) supports the liberal tradition. |
| **Palestine/Jerusalem Post, from 1932; renamed 1950** | English-language Zionist daily; earlier Labor-sympathetic/left, later centre-right. [JTA’s 2000 account](https://www.jta.org/archive/behind-headlines-jerusalem-post-grapples-with-labor-editorial-disputes) documents the shift after the 1989 takeover. |

For 1949 pair Maariv/Haaretz with the relevant party paper; for 1977–1992 start with Maariv plus Haaretz/Yedioth. Party papers establish their own party’s stated position; cross-check other claims. Prefer two independent reports; one wire dispatch printed twice counts once. Judge a journalist’s sourced dispatch separately from their opinion columns.

[NLI’s Maariv notice](https://www.nli.org.il/he/newspapers/mar/2020/08) says outside-building viewing extends through 1992, with later issues onsite. Other titles differ. Use onsite reading, subscriptions or librarian-supplied copies; no access-block circumvention.
