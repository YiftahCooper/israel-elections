## The main sources, and how to get them

A few terms used below:

- **Interlibrary loan:** your local or university library borrows a book for you from another library.
- **Institutional scholarship:** history written by an institution (such as the Knesset or IDI) about itself or its
  field. It counts as scholarship, not as an official record.
- **Primary record:** the record made at the time by the body that acted, for example a signed coalition agreement.

|---|---|---|

Later volumes are edited by Shamir and Gideon Rahat. | Specialist research on each election and on how the government was formed afterwards. The [INES university bibliography](https://socsci4.tau.ac.il/mu2/ines/book-series/) (INES is the Israel National Election Studies at Tel Aviv University) confirms these volumes exist. We still need to check who wrote each chapter, and the page numbers. | Try an interlibrary loan first. Previews on the publisher's site or Google Books can show which chapters are relevant. Current purchase prices were not checked; ask for a quote. |

## Newspapers: know the era and who owned the paper

A newspaper's political leaning changed with its era and its owner. The table gives a working description for each
paper, with a source for that description. Use the paper's news reports, never its editorials or opinion columns.

| Newspaper and its useful years | Leaning, with a source |
|---|---|
| **Davar, 1925–1996** | The daily of the Histadrut, the general trade-union federation; the voice of the Mapai and later Labor establishment. [National Library of Israel (NLI) title record](https://www.nli.org.il/he/newspapers/dav). |
| **Herut, 1948–1965** | The Herut party's own newspaper. [NLI history](https://www.nli.org.il/he/newspapers/hrt/1949/02). |
| **Al HaMishmar, 1943–1995** | The paper of Hashomer Hatzair and Mapam: the socialist Zionist left. [NLI title history](https://www.nli.org.il/he/newspapers/ahr); we could read it here only through search-engine text. |
| **HaTzofe, 1937–2008** | The paper of the Mizrahi movement and then the National Religious Party (NRP); national-religious in outlook after its sale in 2003. [NLI history](https://www.nli.org.il/he/newspapers/hzh/1955/08). |
| **Maariv, from 1948** | A commercial, independent paper. In its early years it was in the right-wing Zionist tradition and critical of Mapai. [NLI history](https://www.nli.org.il/he/newspapers/mar/2020/08). |
| **Yedioth Ahronoth, from 1939** | A commercial, mass-circulation paper with varied voices; no permanent left or right label. Ask a librarian how to reach old issues. |
| **Haaretz, from 1919** | Independent and liberal; in modern times its editorial position is generally on the Zionist left. [An NLI account](https://blog.nli.org.il/tag/הארץ/) supports the liberal tradition. |
| **The Palestine Post, from 1932, renamed the Jerusalem Post in 1950** | An English-language Zionist daily. It leaned to Labor and the left at first, and later to the centre-right. [JTA's 2000 account](https://www.jta.org/archive/behind-headlines-jerusalem-post-grapples-with-labor-editorial-disputes) (JTA is the Jewish Telegraphic Agency, a news agency) documents the shift after the paper was taken over in 1989. |

How to use them:

- **For 1949**, pair Maariv or Haaretz with the newspaper of the party in question.
- **For 1977–1992**, start with Maariv plus Haaretz or Yedioth.
- **A party's own paper** proves only that party's own stated position. Anything it says about others needs checking
  elsewhere.
- **Prefer two independent reports.** A wire service (a news agency) sells one story to many papers; one wire
  dispatch printed in two papers counts only once.
- **Judge a journalist's reporting and opinions separately.** A sourced news dispatch by a journalist can be good
  evidence even when the same journalist's opinion columns are not.

**Access.** [NLI's notice about Maariv](https://www.nli.org.il/he/newspapers/mar/2020/08) says issues up to 1992 can
be viewed from outside the library building; later issues only on site. Other newspapers have different rules. Use
on-site reading, subscriptions, or copies supplied by a librarian. We never get around access blocks.

## Applying this to our actual gaps

Each row is a question the app cannot yet answer. The middle column says what to read first and, if that does not
settle it, which record would.

| Open question | What to read first; what would settle it if still unclear |
|---|---|
| **1992: the recommendations to the President of Labor, Likud, the NRP, United Torah Judaism (UTJ), Moledet and Shas.** After each election the President consults every party, and each party says whom it recommends to form the government. Six of these 1992 recommendations are missing. | Read the government-formation discussion in the 1992 *Elections in Israel* volume, then the six party histories in Kenig and Rahat. Search Maariv and Haaretz for late June to early July 1992. Ask for President Chaim Herzog's June 1992 consultation file. We need a statement naming each party; a total, or a later confidence vote, cannot fill in six missing recommendations. |
| **1988: Hadash, the Progressive List for Peace and the Arab Democratic Party.** Whether, and on what conditions, these three parties recommended a candidate. | Borrow the 1988 volume. Read its chapters on the Arab parties and on government formation, and the press of 7–13 November 1988. The President's House consultation records would settle any conditional recommendations. A chapter that gives only the 63–55 totals is not enough. |
| **1981: the date** | Keep two dates apart: the date the President gave the mandate to form a government (disputed between 15 and 16 July) and the formation of government 19 on 5 August. The *Elections 1981* volume gives background. President Navon's letter, in the Israel State Archives (ISA) file **נ-571/13, pages 5–7**, and his diary, file **נ-224/4**, would settle the disputed date. Ask a librarian or archivist for copies; do not guess from the dates newspapers were printed. |
| **1949: the Yemenite Association** (was it a member of the first government?) | Compare Galnoor and Blander, page 25, Figure 1.1, with IDI's account. Request the already-identified article by Gruweis Kovalsky in *Israel Affairs* (2021) through a library, and the newspaper *Herut* of 11 March 1949. The signed agreement or the record of joining would settle membership; a confidence vote alone does not. |
| **1977: Flatto-Sharon** (was this one-man list part of the coalition?) | Borrow *The Elections in Israel 1977*. Read the press of June 1977 and any deposited agreements for an explicit, continuing pledge of support. A 2018 obituary and a vote for the government at its swearing-in cannot by themselves settle coalition membership. |

## Practical order

1. Use the free Kenig and Rahat PDF today.
2. Borrow *Elections 1992*, *Elections 1988* and the Galnoor–Blander *Handbook* first.
3. Buy the ₪98 Hebrew party book if a physical copy helps.
4. Consider buying the two election volumes only after checking their contents, whether they can be borrowed, and
   their real prices.

**How to get help from a library.** Get a public-library card or a National Library card. Send a librarian the five
rows above, with the archive file numbers. Ask for interlibrary loans, on-site newspapers, and copies of articles
supplied lawfully. Keep any fact that is still unresolved marked as unknown or disputed: a book appearing in a
catalogue never raises our certainty about a fact.
