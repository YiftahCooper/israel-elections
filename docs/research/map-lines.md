# Locality map lines — source and depiction decisions

**As of 7 November 2026: eight independently controlled cartographic layers ship.** They are the pre-1967 armistice lines in Judea and Samaria, historical no-man’s-land, Mount Scopus, the present Israel–Gaza land boundary, Egypt and Jordan frontiers, the Lebanon Blue Line and Golan Alpha. **Golan Bravo, Alpha-1 and an exact area-of-separation polygon do not ship.** The security barrier also remains out. Provider boundaries stay hidden.

The decision criterion is a defensible **cartographic depiction with correct identity, topology and an explicit source/date**, not a cadastral or boundary-pillar survey. Coarse sources are not snapped to detailed sources and different historical lines are not averaged. A numerical difference alone does not establish a historical adjustment. Every on-map explanation states the scope; the snapshot date is a retrieval date, not a claim that a boundary was resurveyed that day.

**Natural Earth “10m” means 1:10 million, not ten-metre resolution.** Its several-kilometre deviations are reported, not hidden or treated as evidence that a treaty boundary moved. ## Every >100 m stretch and cropped comparison

[The disagreement atlas](map-line-stretches.md) lists **338 continuous source portions grouped into 114 nearby crops**, with start/end coordinates, direction, worst measured point, maximum and depiction decision. It covers both directions for Judea and Samaria and Gaza. Adjacent portions within 150 m share a crop; none is silently discarded. Raw records are retained in [discrepancy-stretches.json](../../data/raw/map-lines/discrepancy-stretches.json).

The source lines are clipped against a 100 m buffer of the other product. The buffer’s polygon approximation is below 0.008 m; each retained portion is segmentized to ≤10 m, retaining the original vertices. The >100 m inclusion step is continuous-line clipping, not just point sampling. Maxima remain sampled values with the stated ≤5 m sampling gap. Coordinates locate comparisons and are not survey control.

### What the regional explanations do and do not establish

- **Mount Scopus:** a missing closed feature causes the 2.37 km raw maximum. It is not legitimate to shift that enclave toward the main OCHA line to reduce a distance metric.
- **Latrun:** paired front lines bound an area, not a single centreline. OCHA and OSM generalize the edges differently. The hatch follows the closed OSM ring; both existing line edges remain visible. No centroid, buffer strip or interpolated compromise is substituted.
- **Jerusalem:** UN map 229 explicitly distinguishes Israeli and Jordanian lines, including the gap between them. The app preserves paired-line topology. The Government House / Ramat Rachel / Beit Safafa sector is not flattened into one straight OCHA segment. A particular later rectification is not inferred solely from the distance field.
- **Wadi Ara / Rhodes arrangements:** Article VI of the signed agreement specifies staged establishment of the Iraqi-sector line, including Wadi Ara. Articles VI(11) and IX permit agreed rectifications. Both modern products already purport to depict the settled pre-1967 system; their residual offset alone does not identify one as a pre-arrangement military front. No claim that every northern bend is a later legal adjustment is made.
- **Ordinary stretches:** many >100 m differences are outside enclaves and no-man’s-land. Different vertex density and local bends are observable; the specific historical cause is often **not established**. The selected complete OSM cartographic product is used consistently. This is a documented cartographic choice, not proof that every OSM vertex is legally exact.

### Scanned map inspection and registration

Retained primary material is in `data/raw/map-lines/scans/`: UN Treaty Series volume 42 (including the signed annex reproductions), UN map 229 (November 1949), UNDOF map 2916 Rev.131 (March 2024), and the full UNIFIL map 4144 Rev.69 (March 2026). The portrait thumbnail on the UNIFIL page crops the route; the complete original map was used instead. The signed national 1949 annex is 1:500,000; it cannot establish metre-level alignment. The volume’s monochrome Jerusalem scan is much less legible than the separate colour UN map.

The [Jerusalem registration](map-line-checks/jlm-georef.png) uses four approximate monument-symbol centres on the retained 1400 px map: Holy Sepulchre, Dome of the Rock, David’s Tomb and Western Wall. [Control coordinates and affine coefficients](map-line-checks/jlm-control.json) are retained. Internal fitted residuals are 0.6–9.3 m, **not absolute map accuracy**: symbol centres, monument reference positions, historical drawing and raster resolution introduce additional uncertainty. No production geometry was digitized, snapped or corrected from this fit. It is a topology/route check, not a legal adjudication.

The [Golan registration](map-line-checks/golan-georef.png) uses four latitude/longitude graticule intersections. [Its control record](map-line-checks/golan-georef-control.json) is retained. It distinguishes Alpha from the 1949 armistice line and exposes the NE overlay-limit generalization; it does not justify filling an exact separation polygon.

## Gaza: choose the feature before choosing coordinates

The shipped feature is the **present cartographic Israel–Gaza Strip land boundary**, independently compared with the UN global boundary product (372.9 m sampled maximum; possible shared upstream cartography remains unknown), not an “original 1949 Green Line”. This layer follows the Israel-facing edge; the Gaza–Egypt frontier and coastline are outside its scope. S/1471 defines local patrol zones A, B and C in Palestine Belt grid coordinates. Its Article III preserves the parties’ positions regarding the armistice demarcation line. “1950 adjustment” must not be presented as a single, unqualified new international border.

As a location check, the northern A-zone path’s stated references convert (EPSG:28191 → WGS84) from roughly 34.49053° E, 31.59473° N to 34.54643° E, 31.51207° N; the southern C-zone references lie roughly 31.36218°–31.27908° N. The largest OCHA/OSM discrepancy is around **34.4996° E, 31.5025° N**, south of the northern path and far north of the southern one. Therefore the 922 m maximum is not explained merely by comparing 1949 and 1950 northern lines. Exact signed maps govern any reconstruction; the app does not invent that historical overlay from a text-only conversion.

No claim of exhaustive survey validation is made. Dataset ownership does not prove independence of original surveys. No English Wikipedia article was used. Commons hosts the inspected **UN-authored map**, whose source identity remains UN map 229.

GovMap was opened normally in the browser. Public layer search for `הקו הירוק` returned no result; a catalog data request did not yield an exportable layer. No account token was copied and no protected layer was scraped. UN pages that returned plain 403 to earlier fetches were not retried with header tricks: normal browser access worked for the agreement and current mission pages. A stale dated UNDOF URL returned “Page Not Found”; the canonical page is recorded above.

## Reproduction and licences

The source geometries, selected regional cross-checks, source images, control parameters and generated production GeoJSON are retained; no future download is required. Node dependencies remain in the project lockfile. Geometry/plotting tools are pinned in `data/raw/map-lines/research-requirements.txt` (Python 3.14; Shapely 2.2.0, pyproj 3.8.0, NumPy 2.5.4, Matplotlib 3.11.2, Pillow 13.0.0). Research dependency copies and caches belong under `.backup-scratch/`, not in durable data or docs.

- `scripts/build-map-lines.py`: rebuilds `public/data/map-lines.geojson` from the retained OSM sources, extracting existing closed rings without inventing connectors.
- `scripts/audit-map-lines.py`: recreates continuous >100 m intervals and comparison crops under `.backup-scratch/v4/`.
- `scripts/archive-map-line-audit.py`: selects those accepted crops, writes the coordinate/decision atlas and retains the audit records.
- `scripts/compare-map-lines.py`: recreates the matched-sector UN/NE comparisons using spatial-indexed point-to-segment distances.
- `scripts/register-jerusalem-map.py` and `scripts/register-golan-map.py`: recreate the diagnostic registrations. They do not alter production geometry.
- `scripts/check-map-line-candidates.mjs`: retains the earlier raw whole-extract screening; its unmatched-feature maxima must not be relabelled as ordinary positional errors.

Production boundary coordinates are derived from OSM, © OpenStreetMap contributors, [ODbL](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright); required OSM credits remain visible on the map. UN/OCHA and NE vectors are retained research cross-checks, not alternate production boundary layers. Natural Earth data is public domain; UN source maps/data keep their original publisher attribution. Diagnostic modified map crops are labelled as registrations and do not carry UN branding as an endorsement.

Exact public vector inputs include OSM relation APIs 7304999, 1473938, 1473946 and 184818; UN query layer 0 at the international-boundary service linked above with `geometry=33,29,36.5,34.5`, `inSR=4326`, `outSR=4326`, `outFields=*`, and `f=geojson`; and the Natural Earth GeoJSON linked above. Every shipped feature has a layer ID, source, snapshot date and original OSM way ID where applicable. Derived ring features name their source relation.

## On-screen copy and limits

Final bilingual labels/explanations, source links and snapshot text are retained directly in `src/app/locality-map-lines.ts`; the legend renders them without a separate copy of the text. Sources are behind each (i), labels remain short, and each category has its own checkbox. Hatching is in a separate layer beneath election dots. Provider boundaries cannot double or contradict these layers because their style layers remain removed. Golan limitations are a short bilingual label with the explanation behind (i), not a blank global “boundaries unavailable” state.
