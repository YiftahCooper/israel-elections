# Joint lists, blocs and colours, 1949–1977: research log

Research done on **4 November 2026** for `data/meta/k1.json` to `data/meta/k9.json`. The
file format is described in [data/meta/README.md](../../data/meta/README.md). This document records the
method, coverage, conflicts between sources and remaining gaps. These files are research data: no
dataset in `public/data/` was changed, and the app does not read them yet.

**Source rule.** English-language Wikipedia is not used anywhere (owner rule, 4 November 2026). It was
consulted for colour cross-checks before that rule was given. Those copies were deleted, and nothing
from them reached the output files: a search of `data/meta/` finds no `en.wikipedia` reference.

## Verdict

- **Joint lists.** Every elected member of every joint list in 1949–1977 is assigned to a constituent
  party, apart from one member (1977 Hadash, rank 5, Hanna Mwais), for whom no source states a party.
  274 dataset ranks are assigned across 13 joint lists; 2 assignments (1977 Likud ranks 21 and 24) and
  one rank-29 identity (1969) are medium confidence. 1973 (k8), which has no dataset, is covered by
  name and Knesset `PersonID` (96 members of four joint lists).
- **Blocs.** Each election has two schemes: the first government at its formation (coalition,
  opposition and main opposition) and Mapai/the Alignment with its affiliated Arab lists. All nine
  coalition seat totals match the familiar figures (73, 65, 80, 86, 68, 75, 102, 68, 61).
- **Colours.** No official, ballot or campaign colour source was found for this era. All colours are
  the Hebrew Wikipedia convention, labelled secondary, with confidence at most `medium`.

**Lists deliberately not split.** These were single parties at the time, mostly mergers: the NRP
(ב, 1959 onwards; Mizrachi and Hapoel HaMizrachi merged in 1956–57, OData factions 982 → 981), the
1961 Liberal Party, Ahdut HaAvoda–Poalei Zion, the 1968 Israeli Labor Party (its Mapai, Ahdut HaAvoda
and Rafi wings are not separated), La'am, the Democratic Movement for Change (יש 1977, "התמזגו למפלגה
חדשה"), and the 1977 United Arab List (no source found describing it as an alliance). Lists named
"… ובלתי מפלגתיים" (and non-party members) are treated as single-party lists.

## Method

1. **Rosters.** The elected rosters of each joint list in `public/data/kNN.json` were matched to Knesset
   persons through `KNS_PersonToPosition` (Knesset numbers 1–9, MK positions 43/61, faction position 54)
   and `KNS_Person`. Hebrew name order, titles and maiden or adopted names needed manual overrides, all
   confirmed through the person's faction membership in that Knesset, for example 1949 rank 1
   "פישמן יהודה לייב" = Maimon (2460), 1955/59 "רוזנברג ישראל שלמה" = Ben-Meir (2203), 1977 "ציפורי",
   "דייקסל" and "קאופמן" = Tzipori-Hendin (2573), Dekel (2285) and Kaufman (2577).
2. **Party membership.** For each joint list, Hebrew Wikipedia's member list by party (secondary) was
   mapped onto dataset ranks. Each member was then checked against official evidence:
   - the Knesset website biography (`/WebSiteApi/knessetapi/MKs/GetMkPublicActivity?mkId=<SiteId>`,
     where `SiteId` comes from OData `KNS_MkSiteCode`), quoted where it names the party;
   - OData faction membership, the strongest evidence where a joint faction later split: Torah Religious
     Front in 1960 (factions 215/233) and 1977 (361/371), Mapam's two-day split in April 1977 (999),
     plus the member's own-party faction in another Knesset (for example Mapai 251 or Ahdut HaAvoda 261
     in Knesset 5).
   No assignment contradicts an official record. All 274 assigned members were checked by keyword search of their biographies and faction records; cases flagged as missing or conflicting were reviewed by hand. Members whose biography
   names no party and who have no faction evidence are `medium`.
3. **Gazette scans.** The Israel Democracy Institute hosts scans of the official candidate gazettes
   for each list. Several of them carry handwritten party marks. Inspected: 1965 Alignment (YP 1222,
   ranks 1–56), 1969 Alignment (YP 1563, ranks 20–41) and 1977 Likud (YP 2319, ranks 1–43). The marks
   agree with Hebrew Wikipedia except where noted below.
4. **Governments.** The swearing-in date and ministers come from OData (`GovernmentNum`). Ministers were
   tabulated by their faction on that date. Coalition lists without ministers, mainly the Mapai-affiliated
   Arab lists and Agudat Yisrael in 1977, come from Hebrew Wikipedia's government articles.
5. **Colours.** Row colours from Hebrew Wikipedia's results table for each election, plus the colour
   legends in its list articles for constituent parties.

The scratch caches (OData pulls, biographies, Hebrew Wikipedia raw text, IDI scans) are in
`.backup-scratch/` and are not retained. The generator scripts are kept in
[meta-1949-1977-tools/](meta-1949-1977-tools/) for reference. To regenerate, re-fetch the inputs listed
there (all public, no credentials).

## Gaps

- **Hanna Mwais (1977 Hadash, rank 5):** no source states a constituent party; left unassigned.
- **Candidates below the elected ranks:** not covered, because the k1–k9 rosters stop at the last
  elected rank. The IDI-annotated scans could support later ranks of the 1965 and 1977 lists if those
  rosters are ever extended.
- **k8 ranks:** members are given by name and PersonID only, waiting for a k8 roster.
- **Support-only coalition members** (Arab lists; Agudat Yisrael in 1977) rest on Hebrew Wikipedia.
  The Knesset website's government pages, which list coalition factions, are behind bot protection
  and were not fetched; no circumvention was attempted.
- **Pre-election blocs:** apart from the joint lists themselves (the URF, Gahal, the Alignment, the
  Likud), no formal pre-election bloc between separate lists was found for this era. Lists in surplus
  agreements are already in the datasets. Left/right/religious "camps" are analytical groupings without
  a clear source for this era, so they are not included.
- **Official colours:** none found. A search for campaign material found posters (National Library
  election collection, Farkash Gallery) but no documented party colours; the National Library's
  election archive is the lead if official colours are needed.
