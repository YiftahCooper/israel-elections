# Learn, Play and election history: decision brief

Updated 7 November 2026 against `754c93bdc88e52ae7c00684052522b21dbc2c151`. Supersedes the 5 November draft at `f7dd4f6` (which inspected `e05bf98`). Research and proposals only; no navigation change is approved here. [Plain-language edition](learn-vs-play-owner.md).

**Keep Play as the normal new-visit entrance. Keep explanations available inside it. Do not decide that every non-editing page is a lesson.** There are three user questions: “What happened?”, “How does it work?” and “What if I change it?”. Two entrances can serve three questions, but their names and data context must make that clear.

**Changed since 5 Nov:** the owner now explicitly questions whether history belongs under Learn, authorises an After-the-election tab now, requires wider post-election context, and accepts keeping pending events off the public page. The earlier recommendation to omit reactions is no longer current. Expert leaning away from Learn remains a question, not a decision. Nothing authorises building the Learn/Play split yet.

## Decisions, proposals and limits

- **Owner decisions:** open in Play; improve pages without waiting for this debate; concise labels with sourced bilingual (i) help; keep tools findable; preserve unsaved work; implement After the election as a tab now, with placement revisitable; pending term events stay off the page. The owner's verbatim messages are kept privately, not in this repository.
- **Owner requests still requiring design:** explain the split fully; distinguish historical context from mechanism lessons; reconsider family/mixed-list graphics and vote assumptions; broader post-election reactions, negotiations and relevant polling; editable two-sided Compare and copying across elections. These are requests, not claims of existing features.
- **Recommendation:** retain the two-entrance option, but call its second entrance **“Results & explanations”** in testing, alongside “Learn”. If people reliably distinguish history from lessons, the three-entrance option may be better. Keep the one-workspace option as the strongest continuity alternative. No usability study has selected a winner.
- **Scope:** current repository source, owner messages from 3–7 Nov, the earlier review page question 6, and the After-the-election proposal. The old external comparison is retained below as dated research, not newly verified live behaviour. No audience, adoption or learning-effect claims are made.

### Can users change what the Localities map shows?

**Yes, its presentation and derived group totals; no, not the retained local vote counts.** This corrects the overly broad “Historical record” classification on 5 Nov.

1. The tab loads `data/<election>-localities.json` using the election ID; the table's historical names come from `baselineOf(ds)`. Table totals and local list details use `row.votes`, not edited national totals (`Panels.tsx:146–180,191–204`; loader `Turnout.tsx:24–36`). Coverage begins at k16/2003; earlier elections show national-only, not zero local votes.
2. The map receives both historical data **and current scenario** (`Panels.tsx:179`). Its own notice says **“Historical votes · current grouping”** (`LocalityMap.tsx:228`). Current list/bloc names and colours, grouping and selected list/bloc view affect map interpretation (`LocalityMap.tsx:104–106,224–230`). Search, address lookup, location selection, pan and zoom also change the view, without changing votes.
3. `src/app/locality-map-model.ts:16–36` maps each historical list column onto current lists by `originIds`. Merged lists combine columns. Split descendants share a column using **current national vote proportions** (equal shares if their national total is zero). Bloc mode distributes a list's local votes using its **current elected-seat bloc proportions**, or its list bloc when it has no seats. Therefore list structure, bloc assignments and even national vote/rule edits that change these weights can alter map winners, margins or colours. This is a modelled interpretation, not measured local party ballots. `localityWinner` at `38–45` chooses the derived winner and scenario colour.
4. **No per-kalpi vote editor or persisted local-vote override is present in these UI/model paths.** The coordinates are locality reference points, not polling-station positions (`LocalityMap.tsx:268`). This conclusion is bounded to the inspected current repository, not a promise about future features.
5. Turnout groups select historical locality rows, sum their electorate/ballots/vote mix, then emit **national `addVoters` allocations**; the action carries no local row overrides (`src/model/voterGroups.ts:42–69,74–93`; apply button `Turnout.tsx:124`). A simple unsplit list's local ballot total does not rise when group turnout rises. **Indirect map changes remain possible** if resulting national votes/seats change the split/bloc weights just described. Do not say “Turnout never changes the map” without this qualification.
6. Double-envelope votes cannot be attributed to localities. Unplaced/ambiguous localities retain their votes; missing geographic metadata is not missing participation. 2006 name matching and national/local reconciliation qualifications remain visible. See `Panels.tsx:204,214`, `LocalityMap.tsx:54–61,268,277`.

**Confirmed edge case, not a design choice:** when every descendant of a split historical list has zero national votes, `voterGroups.ts:33–39` assigns the historical column in full to each descendant, while `locality-map-model.ts:21–25` divides it equally. A source column of 100 produces Turnout weights `[100,100]` but map values `[50,50]` for two zero-vote descendants. With other lists present this can skew the normalised group mix. A scratch Node assertion against the pinned implementation reproduced both values; this is not a local-vote edit and does not invalidate the historical table. Flag it for a separately scoped fix; do not promise identical mapping assumptions in this edge case. No application change is included here.

**Latest owner map direction (7 Nov; separate map-work lane):** use the selected open basemap with our CBS-matched locality names; retain accurate, explained armistice/security or unsecured-zone lines rather than the older suggestion to remove land boundaries. A correctly sourced security-barrier line is a low-priority suggestion. Hebrew names must be those familiar to Israeli Hebrew speakers, including Judea/Samaria/Gaza; neighbouring-country names are not the concern. These choices do not turn the map into an editor of local votes, and are not asserted as implemented at the pinned revision.

**Proposed policy:** offer Published map and Current grouping, label estimates beside grouped results, preserve the historical table in both, and never label a derived winner “official”. A future true geographic sandbox would require explicit local overrides, reconciliation with national totals, envelope handling, provenance, reset/undo/share semantics and tests; it is outside this decision. The old speculative demographic/kalpi research remains parked, not suddenly validated by locality aggregates. The wider polling window is also separate from Learn/Play: the owner requested its own window, reliable-polling elections only, pollster toggles in settings, publication-ban explanation and a private evidence store; neither a lesson nor aftermath polling delivers all of that.

## What changed since the old draft

- What if… was insufficiently distinguished from live draft analysis: its cards start from published inputs and create a fresh scenario when opened, rather than adding a change to the active draft.
- Localities was misclassified as wholly historical; the map uses the scenario grouping and structural/seat-weight estimates. National turnout changes have no local write path, but can affect those weights indirectly.
- After the election, its four narrative sections, graphics, polling, sourcing, direct-PM exceptions and release decisions were omitted from the navigation options. It is still not an app tab at this checkout.
- Today's owner answer supersedes “omit reactions”: include election/negotiation context and relevant post-election polling; do not reduce the request to acceptance-of-results quotes or major laws.
- The earlier owner review's “About this election” and glossary entrance were not fully mapped. These are proposed full destinations, not additional existing registered tabs: the About popover and local glossary help already supply some of their content.
- The old draft lacked the verbatim chronology, the latest family and Expert questions, and a complete account of the coordinator's earlier proposal.
- Updated app facts: manual walkthrough; refreshed analysis pages; local help and per-item settings; four level choices; workspace/draft persistence; Compare previous-election default; separate appearance, analysis and result changes. Some Custom/persistence ideas already existed by 5 Nov; this is an updated inventory, not a claim they were all introduced later.
- Current changes distinguish **appearance** (colours/display names), **analysis** (custom grouping/coalition inspection) and **result inputs**. A customised colour or grouping is not automatically a changed election result. `src/model/editScopes.ts:2,66–71`, `src/app/derive.ts:370`, top CHANGELOG entries. The old “selection is hypothetical” wording needed this distinction.
- Existing sourced About context and the read-only separate PM-ballot views needed coverage beyond the tab inventory. A full About destination is proposed; the header popover already exists.
- The community scenario gallery was missing from the old inventory: its GitHub publication/browse/likes UI exists, although the broader shared-settings marketplace is not thereby delivered.
- Share links encode scenario inputs, not a lesson/tab route; future direct destinations require new routing and compatibility, not simply moving tabs.

### 1. Two entrances — provisional recommendation

Top bar: brand, election, two named areas, language/theme/settings. Play also has named draft, changes, undo/redo, save/share and level. The factual area says “Published results · year/status” and “Resume [draft]”; it must not show a draft-delta banner as if history changed. Use one set of view components with explicit immutable-baseline or active-draft inputs. Read-only tie controls are removed; group-history definitions remain visible interpretations. “Try this” opens the exact matching Play party/step/election.

Benefit: one simple distinction between finding facts/explanations and trying changes. Cost: “Learn” is an imperfect label for history, repeated view contexts need careful titles, and casual users may overlook experiments from a factual page. Test “Learn · Results & how elections work”, “Results & explanations” and task labels rather than assuming the wording is settled. No need for separate sites or datasets.

### 2. Three entrances — strongest history distinction

Play owns experiments/analysis; Learn owns system lessons; Results owns records/history. Top bar has three named destinations; scenario controls appear only in Play; both other areas show publication context and Resume draft. History, locality data and After the election no longer need to be called education.

Benefit: best semantic fit to the owner's 7 Nov concern. Cost: another choice on phones, more boundaries between results and their allocation explanation, and more opportunity for election mismatches. Choose if users repeatedly look for historical records in the wrong half of a two-part design. Expert users still need exact law/source explanations.

### 3. One workspace — strongest continuity

Use Board / Experiment / Analyse / Understand & verify task groups. Keep scenario controls, but show per-page context: published history and sources never inherit a misleading scenario-delta caption. Walkthrough and map have explicit Published / Your scenario or Current grouping choices. Rules separates law explanation, editable assumptions and personal settings. “Explain this” stays beside every affected input/result.

Benefit: least navigation upheaval and the shortest experiment/explanation loop. Cost: weakest service distinction; context labels must do more work, and the surrounding draft controls can still confuse users on factual pages. Choose if people understand the facts correctly but mode switching interrupts them. An explicit factual view still must not reset the draft.

## Rules common to all options

**Levels and modes:** Simple/Standard/Expert/Custom control detail, not truth, audience entitlement or which service exists. Keep tools discoverable with in-place More options and a full task index. Custom visibility is already real, not a proposed fifth mode; protect truth labels and Overview. Expert may open deeper traces, fewer introductory cues and higher precision, while Simple gets short labels and rounded display. Neither may remove qualifications or sourced help. An Expert-default-to-Play preference is an optional later proposal; automatically hiding Learn from experts would conflate expertise and intention. Today's request for level-relevant settings is compatible with this, without duplicating settings systems.

**Help and teaching:** existing `Info` popovers and bilingual glossary data (`src/app/components/ui.tsx:10–43,136–147`, `src/app/glossary-data.ts`) are a foundation, not proof that a full glossary page exists. Keep (i), source links and “Explain this scenario” inside Play at every level. Explain the actual threshold, quota, remainder, agreement, transfer assumption or changed seat, not just a generic dictionary entry. The owner asked for clickable explanations of family history changes and the parties behind them. Add a glossary index and worked examples as proposed content; no forced tutorial, timed autoplay, duplicated paragraphs or lesson gate before use. A lesson can have a labelled disposable example with adjustable assumptions, provided its local state is separate from both the published result and the working draft. “Try this in Play” deliberately creates or resumes a scenario; moving an example control must not silently edit either the historical record or the saved experiment. This is a teaching-content proposal, not a shipped feature.

**Three kinds of change:** official/published identity, personal appearance and custom analysis can coexist. Do not tint history as a different result because someone recoloured Likud or inspected a coalition. Mark actual user input locally; label grouped/estimated displays and personal definitions without claiming the votes changed. Distinguish the source of a fact, a classification and an assumption. Exact arithmetic does not measure voter intention.

**Drafts and bridges:** entering a factual page never dispatches a reset or changes draft election/rules/lottery. “Try it” carries election, party/family and lesson step. If an edited draft already exists there, offer Continue named draft / New from published result, retain the former, and return to it in one action. A factual election selector must not silently switch the working draft. Preserve unsaved history, saved versions, reference/comparison and undo/redo; availability failures should explain what cannot be opened without losing the previous page.

**URLs and persistence — existing vs proposed:** current `shareLink` is a compressed `#s=` payload of election, name/description, baseline build, faction-counting baseline and action log (`src/app/store.ts:412–425`). It does **not** encode Learn/Play, tab, glossary term, map viewport or lesson step. Startup restores workspace, then opens a share payload, then falls back to k26 (`App.tsx:95–100`); a restored visit is not always a fresh Play launch. Scenario opening consumes the share hash (`store.ts:326–338`). Workspace restoration and drafts exist (`store.ts:526–588`); do not describe unsaved work as necessarily lost on reload.

Proposed routing must independently identify area, published/draft context, election and destination/step. Retain old `#s=` links, imports, saved IDs and baseline-version warnings; never interpret a historical link as permission to replace a draft. Share a lesson as a lesson, a grouped map with its assumptions, or a scenario with its edits; do not silently publish browser-local preferences/definitions. The existing community gallery stays under Scenarios in Play in all three options. Opening a community scenario must preserve drafts; publication must explain exactly which scenario/description/tags become public and retain its GitHub-account requirement. A lesson/example catalogue should distinguish authored examples from user hypotheses, source facts separately and never endorse an entry by popularity. Broader settings/voter-group marketplace support remains a separate scope. Embedded family actions and separately exported family/voter-group presets need explicit contracts; a scenario link is not a backup of the whole browser catalogue. Browser Back/Forward, reload and old tab redirects must preserve intent. Changing names/placement is a migration task, even with one renderer.

**Phones and interaction:** persistent compact area choices plus readable sibling task names/full index; retain election/context and return-to-draft actions. Proposed wrapped/grouped task rows revise DESIGN.md's single sideways tab strip and need testing. Avoid hiding the whole feature inventory behind icons or submenus; avoid adjacent redundant facts; no jumps when help/loading/edits appear. Hebrew text mirrors, charts/maths remain left-to-right. Bilingual copy, both themes, keyboard focus/touch and unavailable-data states need validation; no accessibility conformance claim is made.

## Decision questions and checks

Only the owner can choose whether the secondary entrance promises teaching or factual reference, whether a third entrance earns its space, and whether Expert should default to a different emphasis. Code determines what is mutable; it cannot decide these preferences.

Before choosing, give a novice and an experienced user an edited draft. Ask them to find a historical local vote, explain the published last seat, explain the draft's last seat, compare with a previous election, inspect a family definition, find what happened after the election, try a lesson's change and recover the original draft. Ask what is factual, interpretive and assumed at each step. Repeat Hebrew/English and phone, provisional/missing data, direct-PM years, splits/merges and old share links. Failure includes wrong-context answers or lost work, even if navigation succeeds. No such usability test was performed for this update.
