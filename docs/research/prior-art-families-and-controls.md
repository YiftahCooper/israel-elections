## What to avoid

- **Vote-flow implications.** A split or merger is an organizational relationship. Differences in national vote totals do not identify individual switchers: turnout, new voters, deaths, migration, changing alliances and abstention can all change the totals. Do not draw vote-width ribbons from one election's party to the next from these data. Even constituency gains in UK examples do not establish voter transfers.
- **False precision inside joint lists.** Candidate affiliations and seats held by constituent parties do not measure their shares of the joint ballot. A 60/40 roster split does not justify a 60/40 vote split. Never put the same joint-list total into two families and then stack them as a partition of 100%.
- **An inevitable-family narrative.** “Social democracy”, organizational descent and a one-election coalition are distinct. A predecessor can have several successors; a party can join different allies at different elections. Preserve dated branches and counterexamples instead of forcing a single rooted tree.
- **Misleading zeroes and interpolation.** Distinguish did-not-contest, missing source, unresolved attribution and measured zero. Plot election points at real dates; lines connect observations, not continuous polling estimates. In 2019, April and September need separate targets.
- **Hidden selection tricks.** Ctrl/Shift-only selection, lasso, tiny stepper arrows and hover-only explanations fail as the primary phone path. Checkbox state must remain visible when filters change; “select all results” must not mean all historical parties.
- **Destructive presets.** Hiding must not delete; a built-in update must not mutate a saved custom definition; choosing a family must not rewrite scenario votes or election-specific blocs. Do not label browser storage “saved forever”.
- **Seat transplantation.** National proportional seats are not individually located district seats. A ring around one dot cannot say “this voter/seat moved from Likud to Labour”. Stable list ordinals describe a drawing, not the identity of a seat or candidate across scenarios.
- **Animation as evidence.** Flipping dots are an attractive transition but hide the old state once settled. Do not rely on a flash to explain changes; support static/reduced-motion reading, a table and explicit baseline. Do not use red/green for gains/losses.
- **Copying deployment stacks.** ParlGov's Django/SQLite service, hosted Flourish templates and a poll model are not reasons to add a backend or polling feed to this static React app. Current Grapher code is not MIT-reusable.

## Choosing the visual form

The following is **I**, informed by P1–P4/P9/P10, not a controlled perception experiment. It compares forms against the app's actual questions.

| Form | Best question | Decision and failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Dated line with election markers | How did one selected family's share/votes change? | **Default for one family**, with separate share/count plots. Break at unknown mixed-list attribution; select a marker for exact components and sources. Unsmooth lines; no annual interpolation presented as data. |
| Small multiples, shared scale | How do several selected families compare over time? | **Default for several families.** Desktop grid, phone vertical stack, identical date domain and scales within each metric. Easier to follow identities than many crossed lines; tiny parties may need a clearly labelled optional local scale. OWID's suffrage example actually renders this form. |
| Stacked area | How was the entire valid vote partitioned? | Secondary only for mutually exclusive, exhaustive membership plus Other and a defined joint-list policy. Area thickness lacks a common baseline except the lowest series; missing attribution must not be filled by guesses. Never stack overlapping lineage families. |
| Stacked columns at election dates | What made up a complete election? | Better than continuous areas for discrete elections and changing memberships. A mixed joint list can remain one labelled hatched segment. Still requires exclusivity and a valid-vote denominator. |
| Slope chart | What changed between exactly two elections? | Useful within Compare; direct endpoint labels and exact deltas. Insufficient for all 26 elections and can imply continuity if endpoint mappings are not stated. |
| Bump chart | Which party/family ranked higher? | Optional rank question only. Rank hides magnitude: second place can be 15% or 35%; attach share labels and do not use as the family-history default. |
| Node-link lineage / dated DAG | Which organizations split, merged or contested together? | Separate **Lineage** view, not the quantitative history chart. Fixed-width relation edges, typed/datable source labels, explicit joint-list container; expand a selected neighborhood on phone rather than shrink a national tree. A DAG supports mergers and splits; a strict tree does not. |
| Sankey/alluvial | What quantity flowed between known origins and destinations? | **Reject for inferred historical vote flows.** Could depict a separately sourced, explicitly measured quantity or a declared hypothetical transfer scenario. Width must name that quantity. D3's Sankey input requires link values; organizational links alone do not supply them. |

**Mixed-list drawing contract (I):** if selected organizations A and B contest together as AB, show one enclosing AB ballot-list shape with A/B member chips and one measured vote total. If A alone is selected, show the AB point as unresolved for the strict A series; opening it shows the AB total and why it cannot be divided. If all members are included, the whole AB total can enter the selected union once. If the membership record itself is incomplete, retain uncertainty even when all known members are checked. A whole-list inclusion mode must state that it includes partners. Overlapping families may be compared individually, but their values cannot be added together without union/deduplication.

The challenge pass specifically looked for: ideologically classified “families” masquerading as ancestry; joint-list double counting; actual versus notional data; repeated-year ambiguity; touch-hostile selection; overflow; hidden native controls; and transitions mistaken for persistent differences. ## Method and reproducibility

Read `README.md`, `PRODUCT.md`, `DESIGN.md`, the synthesis and the earlier Israeli report's relevant source locators; checked current component/model files to distinguish existing controls from proposed ones. The five earlier reports were treated as background, not recounted as new observations. The study defines “best” as **fit to one of the four requested tasks, inspectable state/assumptions, useful phone behavior and adaptation to an MIT static app**; product popularity, accessibility conformance and task-time improvements were not measured.

Workstreams were historical identity/aggregation, numeric goals, navigation/presets, and seat comparison; executed by this research agent without further delegation. Browser actions included: historical election loads and calculation; national percentage edit and submit; expanded regional controls and numeric ArrowUp; entity search/check; starting-map change; chart-option labels; and two-year chamber comparison. A successful automation call is not itself proof of all downstream behavior: the ledger states the resulting state actually inspected. Hidden-input locator failures were recorded and distinguished from access denial.

**Browser parameters:** Playwright 1.64.1 from the established repository dependency installation; Chromium build 1248, runtime Chromium 156; Node 24.18.0. Every substantive survey/interaction context used **1440×900** and **390×844**, device scale factor 1, desktop pointer behavior (viewport testing, not a physical-phone/touch usability study), and `User-Agent: israel-elections-explorer/0.1 (+https://github.com/YiftahCooper/israel-elections)`. Each product's initial desktop/phone screenshots used the same session resized; interaction trials used fresh contexts at each size. Locale was not forced. The shared preview was checked once before direct Playwright, but could not set this UA; its default-UA opening was not used as the evidence for any principle.

Screenshots, working scripts and raw observations are **only** in `.backup-scratch/prior-art/families/`, already ignored by Git, and are not committed. That location is disposable working evidence, not a claim that every external backup system excludes it. Durable reproduction instructions are the URLs/actions/parameters and factual outcomes above; reproducing the live pages exactly is not guaranteed as publishers update them. The scratch runner used `chromium.newContext({userAgent, viewport})`, standard page navigation, visible controls and screenshots; it did not inject application data or bypass checks. The process sandbox initially prevented Chromium launch; approved out-of-sandbox execution permitted public browsing. No global temporary-directory setting changed.

No login, paywall or CAPTCHA was bypassed, no newsletter/personal data submitted, and no vendor save/publication performed. Jina tools were not exposed in this session; no Jina evidence is claimed. Search was only discovery; recommendations rely on opened primary product/licence/documentation pages, with explicit inference labels. Google and Observable access failures were left as gaps.
