# Election metadata research, 2009–2026 (k18–k26)

Outputs: `data/meta/k18.json` … `data/meta/k26.json`. The format and the source rules are in [data/meta/README.md](../../data/meta/README.md).
Research date: 4 November 2026.

## Source rule change during the work

Partway through, the owner ruled out **English Wikipedia (en.wikipedia.org)** as a source for anything. Every en.wikipedia
source recorded until then was removed:

- **Colours** were switched from English Wikipedia's `Module:Political party` to Hebrew Wikipedia infobox colours.
- **Affiliations** that rested on English Wikipedia were re-sourced from Hebrew Wikipedia or Knesset OData, or dropped.
  This covers Meretz–New Movement 2009, Green Movement–Meimad 2009, Rivlin 2013, Yachad 2015, and the duplicate
  cross-checks for UTJ 2013/2022 and Hadash–Ta'al 2022.
- **Bloc sources** were re-sourced from the Hebrew Wikipedia government pages, the Hebrew Wikipedia 2026 election page,
  ynet, The Times of Israel and JNS. The April 2019 "pre-election camps" scheme had no other source, so it was removed.

**No en.wikipedia URL remains in `data/meta`.** A grep of all nine files returns zero matches.
English Wikipedia was consulted only before the rule, to locate other sources, such as the Times of Israel URL for April 2019.

**Evidence by period:**

- **2019–2026:** the official gazette prints each candidate's party, so whole rosters are covered (`high`; the two 2026
  rosters from bechirot.fyi are `medium`).
- **2009, 2013, 2015 and 2022:** the gazettes name only list-level sponsors, or none at all for 2009 and 2013. The CEC lists
  on the old Knesset election sites, read through the Wayback Machine for 2009, also have no affiliations. Assignments
  therefore come from:
  - Knesset OData faction splits after the election (official; `high`);
  - Hebrew Wikipedia (`medium`);
  - complement rules where a source states every reserved slot. This applies to Likud–Ahi 2009 (Ahi ranks 39 and 45) and the
    Zionist Union 2015 (Hatnua ranks 2, 8, 16, 21, 24 and 25; "the remaining places went to Labor").

## Search log (condensed)

1. Read the datasets. The k18/k19 rosters have no sponsors. The k20 and k25 rosters have list-level sponsors and no per-candidate
   party. The k21–k24 and k26 rosters carry `partyHe`.
2. Rendered the retained gazettes (`data/raw/k18…k25`) with the repo's pdfjs helper. They confirm there is no candidate-party
   column for 2009–2015 or 2022, and give the 2022 sponsor strings, such as the five Religious Zionism sponsors.
3. **Knesset OData:** pulled all `KNS_Faction` rows and every `KNS_PersonToPosition` row with a faction for Knessets 18–25
   (paged; 406 persons). Splits used:
   - K18: Otzma LeYisrael and Mada.
   - K19: Likud and Yisrael Beiteinu (9 July 2014); Ra'am and Ta'al (22 January 2015).
   - K20: Labor and Hatnua (1 January 2019); Agudat Yisrael and Degel HaTorah, and Ta'al (9 January 2019).
   - K25: RZP, Otzma Yehudit and Noam (20 November 2022); the National Right/New Hope (13 March 2024); Blue and White
     (8 July 2025).

   Also pulled positions by `GovernmentNum` 32–37 to identify the factions holding office when each government was sworn in.
4. **Hebrew Wikipedia**, fetched through the MediaWiki API with back-off after HTTP 429: party pages (member tables per
   Knesset; infobox colours), Knesset pages, election pages 2009–2026, and government pages 32–37.
5. **News:**
   - ynet: 2013 recommendations.
   - Times of Israel: April 2019 recommendations (article body read); 2021 recommendations (headline only; the totals are from Hebrew Wikipedia).
   - JNS: 2020 recommendations.
   - Jerusalem Post: September 2019 recommendations (headline only; the breakdown is from Hebrew Wikipedia).
6. **Cross-check of the official gazette `partyHe` (k21–k24) against Knesset faction splits:**
   - Covered: UTJ, Hadash–Ta'al, Ra'am–Balad, the Joint List, Yamina, Labor–Gesher–Meretz, Religious Zionism, New Hope,
     Atid Ehad and Blue and White.
   - No disagreement in list affiliation.
   - Kamal Mariach is the only mismatch: gazette Israel Resilience, later the Yesh Atid–Telem faction. That is a
     post-election move.
7. **Blocked or unavailable sources:**
   - The Knesset election-site candidate pages (elections18/19) and the main.knesset.gov.il governments pages serve a
     bot challenge. I did not bypass it.
   - The archived 2025 Wayback copies are the challenge page. The 2009 Wayback copies are real but have no affiliations.
   - IDI party PDFs found were platforms, not candidate breakdowns.
   - The Firecrawl connector was not used.

## Conflicts between sources

- **Meretz–New Movement 2009.** English Wikipedia (citing the Jerusalem Post) gave the New Movement ranks 3, 7 and 11.
  Hebrew Wikipedia names Horowitz (3), Sasson (7), Reshef (9) and Mara'ana (12, withdrew), and the gazette roster matches
  those names. Rank 11 is Michal Rozin. The Hebrew account is used.
- **Jewish Home 2015.** A pre-list report of the Tkuma agreement gave ranks 2, 9, 14 and 18. The approved list has Smotrich
  at 8, and Hebrew Wikipedia's member table names only Ariel and Smotrich. Only those two are assigned.
- **Zionist Union 2015:**
  - Hebrew Wikipedia's member table lists Amir Peretz under Labor; he rejoined Labor in 2016. Slot 8 was Hatnua's, and the
    2019 official faction split puts him in Hatnua, so he is assigned to Hatnua.
  - Yael Cohen-Paran (25) held a Hatnua-allotted slot as Green Movement chair. She is assigned `green-movement` (`medium`).
    The official faction record puts her with Hatnua.
- **Ra'am–Ta'al.** In 2009 el-Sana (Mada) is recorded inside Ra'am (UAL), and the list name has two components. In 2013
  the official list name adds Mada as a third component, and el-Sana (rank 5) is `mada`.
- **National Unity 2022.** Hebrew Wikipedia counts Eisenkot (3) and Kahana (9) in Gantz's party. Both joined the alliance in
  August 2022 rather than through a sponsoring party's roster. They are assigned `israel-resilience` with `low` confidence.
- **35th government (2020).** The Blue and White list split three ways:
  - Gantz's faction, including Tamano-Shata (elected in a Yesh Atid slot);
  - Derech Eretz (two Telem-slot MKs);
  - Yesh Atid–Telem, including Mariach (elected in an Israel Resilience slot).

  The scheme gives ranks from official faction membership on 17 May 2020, which yields a 73-seat coalition.
- **32nd government (2009).** Sworn in 31 March 2009. United Torah Judaism joined on 1 April; it is marked `joined`.
  OData shows no UTJ office-holder on day one.
- **Colours.** Hebrew Wikipedia is not internally consistent:
  - The per-Knesset faction tables differ from party infobox headers. Examples: Yisrael Beiteinu `#0096B1` vs `#99C3E1`;
    the Joint List `#01A986` vs `#51B2AB`.
  - The infobox header is used throughout.
  - The Religious Zionism infobox lists new colours "from 2026" while its header still shows the earlier scheme, so k26 ט
    is `low`.
  - Party colours are stable across elections because one page supplies each party.
  - Party colours do not necessarily equal the joint list's colour.

## Gaps

- **Lists not covered:**
  - 2009 Holocaust Survivors with Ale Yarok Graduates (יק);
  - 2013 Otzma LeYisrael (נץ; Hatikva plus Ben-Ari's camp; the constituent registered parties were not established);
  - 2013 Ale Yarok – Liberal List.

  Home and Religious National Israeli (Jewish Home 2022); National Arab Party (Arab List 2015).
- **2013 recommendations:** the positions of Kadima and Balad are unverified, and Likud–Yisrael Beiteinu is placed with its own
  leader without a quote.
- **2015 recommendations:** the Joint List and Yesh Atid are the remainder; their position is not stated in the Hebrew source.
- **2009 recommendations:** the per-party split of the 65 is inferred from the bloc total.
- **2022 pre-election blocs:** only the Netanyahu bloc is sourced. The change/Arab split copies the app's existing assignment.
- **2026:** no government yet; President Herzog's consultations were due to begin on 4 November 2026.
- **No official colour source** was found. CEC ballot material is monochrome, and party brand guides were not located.
  All colours are labelled secondary.

## Reproduction

The JSON files are the curated record: every assignment carries its source, quote and confidence.

- They were generated by a scratch builder (`.backup-scratch/meta/build.py` with `manual.py`, `blocs.py`, `colours.py` and
  `common.py`). That builder reads `public/data`, the downloaded Hebrew Wikipedia wikitext, and the OData pull.
- Each quote recorded from a Hebrew Wikipedia page was checked against the fetched wikitext with markup stripped (165
  fragments, 0 mismatches). Infobox colour lists are shown with their templates rendered and labelled as such.
- The builder validates three things: each named rank matches the expected surname, no rank is assigned twice, and every
  bloc seat total matches the total stated by its source.
