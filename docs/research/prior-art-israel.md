# Israeli election interfaces: hands-on prior-art study

Research date: 4 November 2026. Decision: which familiar Israeli display and interaction conventions this explorer should adopt without sacrificing historical accuracy, neutrality or exact vote allocation.

## Evidence and limits

**Observation (O)** below means inspected page text, browser behaviour or a capture; **inference (I)** means a design judgment, not a measured audience preference. Public availability and appearance in search establish discoverability, **not usage, popularity or trust**. No audience analytics or Israeli user interviews were available; “visitors expect” means a hypothesis supported by recurring interfaces. No ranking by actual traffic is claimed.

Read `README.md`, `PRODUCT.md`, `DESIGN.md` and the relevant components. Built this checkout with `npx vite build`, served it with `npx vite preview --port 5342`, and studied the Hebrew UI with Playwright. Dependencies required `npm install`; installation and browser/port access needed sandbox escalation. The shared preview reported unavailable and then `ERR_CONNECTION_REFUSED`, so installed Playwright was used. Desktop: **1440×900**; phone: **390×844**. Screenshots are local, disposable evidence under `.backup-scratch/prior-art/israel/`, already gitignored. They are not committed or remotely hosted; this document retains the findings and source locators. Repeating a capture may produce different current content.

Browser requests used `israel-elections-explorer/0.1 (+https://github.com/YiftahCooper/israel-elections)`. Jina `search_web`, `read_url` and `capture_screenshot_url` were also used; those tools expose no caller-set User-Agent, so their service-side UA could not be verified. No email address was submitted. No login, subscription, challenge or paywall was bypassed; only normally delivered public content was inspected. No English Wikipedia page was opened or cited. Polimeter itself declares a dependency on it: that is a reason not to import its numbers into this project.

Coverage combines national results, vote calculators, coalition selection, locality maps, polling comparisons and quizzes. Official vote counts across products often derive from the **same CEC dataset**; agreement between them is not independent validation of the election arithmetic. Their layouts and interaction choices are separate design evidence. Haaretz and TheMarker belong to the same publishing family; Mako/N12/its compass are another family. Capture failures and partial rendering remain gaps, not evidence a feature does not exist.

## Domain conventions and where this project differs

| ID / convention | Observed evidence, with counterexamples | Project decision and audience implication (inference) |
|---|---|---|
| C1: Seats answer “who can reach 61?” | Haaretz's builder starts with a 61-seat question; Polimeter shows a coalition total, majority meter and missing-seat count; Kan's 2022 article separates list seats from bloc totals; Globes' election-night report compares camp totals. **Counterexample:** CEC leads with counting/turnout totals and a list table, not blocs. | **Offer both.** Retain the 120-seat chamber and sourced blocs; strengthen a compact `X / 61 · חסרים Y` readout near coalition actions. Do not replace vote counts with headline seat estimates. Confidence high for recurring convention, medium for desired prominence. |
| C2: “תוצאות אמת”, “מדגם”, “סקר” and finality are different states | CEC25 says `תוצאות סופיות רשמיות`; Haaretz labels `לפי ספירת 100% מהקולות`; Globes distinguishes broadcaster exit polls and incoming actual counts; Jeremy dates polls and describes averaging; Kaplan shows time and percentage of ballot boxes. | **Keep, clarify.** The local 2026 warning already distinguishes final count from gazetted publication. Carry election date, source snapshot and scenario status into comparisons and exports. 100% counted must not silently become “official final”. Confidence high. |
| C3: Lists descend by strength, with names plus recognizable identifiers | CEC25 shows full registered names, ballot letters, seats, share and votes in descending strength; the calculator's results do the same seat ordering; Kaplan shows ranked shares; Haaretz's builder ranks lists by seats. CEC relies on letters; Haaretz uses leader imagery/name tokens; the app uses verified logos or ballot slips. | **Keep names, letters and verified period logos; offer sorting.** Keep stable identity across edits, and label whether sorting is by scenario seats, votes or original result. Do not assume logo colour is a universal cross-site standard. Confidence high for ranking/identification, medium for sorting preference. |
| C4: The threshold has its own visible story | Kan has a distinct `המפלגות שלא עברו את אחוז החסימה` section; Globes gives below-threshold percentages beside 3.25%; Kaplan draws a dashed `אחוז החסימה 3.25%` separator; IDI explains the valid-vote denominator. **Counterexample:** CEC retains zero-seat lists in its ordinary table without a dramatic boundary. | **Keep and sharpen.** App's hatched threshold row and share-of-threshold bars are excellent. Add the literal familiar phrase `מתחת לאחוז החסימה` and a source-dependent votes-to-cross action in the row/details; distinguish not qualifying from qualifying but receiving no seat in historical rules. Never hardcode 3.25% for all years. Confidence high. |
| C5: A camp label is an editorial/time-specific grouping, not electoral law | Kan's 2022 article gives Netanyahu 64 and the then-government camp 51, leaving Hadash–Ta'al outside that 51; Jeremy separates an Arab bloc; Ynet exposes an Arab-bloc toggle; CEC has no bloc classifications. Polimeter offers unclassified parties, including Haredi parties, instead of forcing all into two camps. | **Offer both historical government and election-night blocs.** This app's 2022 default is the first government: 64/56, while the pre-election preset gives 64/46/10. Make the selected preset/date prominent. Keep Arab lists individually visible and equally selectable; no automatic exclusion from coalitions. Keep Shas and UTJ distinct, with constituent-party detail where sourced. Confidence high for differing conventions, medium for preferred default. |
| C6: RTL text does not require every graphic to mirror | CEC, Haaretz, Kan, IDI, Madad and Polimeter use Hebrew RTL text. Ynet's document computes LTR while its Hebrew panels align RTL; Kaplan's body also computes LTR while map labels are Hebrew. Fonts vary: Arial, Open Sans, SimplerProH, Assistant, IBM Plex Sans Hebrew, nextexit-variable. | **Keep logical RTL layout and isolated LTR arithmetic.** Avoid treating hemicycle position as actual seating or an ideological scale unless explicitly selected/labeled. Keep the current Hebrew face; there is no evidence a font replacement improves comprehension. Use larger counts and clearer spacing where hierarchy matters. Confidence high for variation, medium for hierarchy proposal. |
| C7: “איך הצביעו ביישוב שלכם?” is a general-interest task | Ynet leads with neighborhood/city search; N12's map article leads with localities and city comparisons; CEC has top navigation for locality and ballot results; Kaplan puts locality search directly on the map. | **Change discovery, keep depth.** Make read-only locality exploration reachable from Simple with an in-place level offer or preview; reserve turnout modelling for Expert. Clearly label this app's locality points as locality reference positions, not ballot boxes or neighborhoods. Confidence high for recurrence, medium for navigation choice. |
| C8: Calculators expose votes, the threshold and surplus partners explicitly | The independent calculator offers historical load, editable names/votes, field/text modes, threshold input, searchable agreement pairs and `חשב`; IDI teaches that an agreement only works when both lists qualify; Jeremy includes surplus agreements alongside poll tables. Media front pages usually omit them. | **Keep live recalculation; add efficient entry.** Offer batch text input at Standard/Expert and searchable partners. Keep the app's actual agreement-effect calculation and disabled/below-threshold explanations. Do not present agreements as vote transfers or coalition commitments. Confidence high for observed controls, medium for adoption priority. |
| C9: Mobile prioritization matters more than literal desktop compression | Calculator stacks form cards; Ynet moves options behind a compact menu; Madad keeps a narrow table but its cookie panel covers much of the first phone screen; Haaretz's article introduction delays the actual builder below the first screen; Mako's first phone capture is an advertisement. | **Keep the app free of these delays.** Preserve its immediate chamber, full-screen edit sheet and horizontal tool strip, but add a direct jump to lists/locality search and a visible close/back affordance. Reaching the lists currently requires scrolling past the chamber and figures. The captured phone header also visibly clips the election selector; prioritize keeping the election/year legible over adding another jump control. Confidence high for captures, medium for proposed task order. |

These conventions do **not** justify coloring a camp as morally good/bad, assuming a fixed left–right ordering for every election, merging Arab parties into one political actor, or making Haredi parties permanently committed to one bloc. The observed products disagree on grouping and colours. The project should expose named, dated interpretations rather than claim one official political map.

## The local app: observed tasks and strengths to keep

Hebrew study ran from **10:13:29 to at least 10:25:17 UTC** (11 minutes 48 seconds, interleaved with external comparison and evidence reading). The session included the 2026 Simple opening; 2022 in Standard; editing Likud to 1,200,000 votes and undoing; coalition baseline selection (64); switching first-government and pre-election blocs; opening the bloc editor and inspecting constituent parties; Expert locality map and Jerusalem search; phone sheets; moving Meretz to 160,000 votes in Simple (4 seats), inspecting the changed candidate cutoff and undoing; surplus-agreement overview; comparison and walkthrough; 1949 in dark mode. This is an interaction study, not an engine audit or WCAG certification.

Concrete comparisons:

- The 2026 opening already combines recognisable lists, chamber, three bloc totals and a 61 marker without ads or a login. CEC gives better raw auditability; media provide more headline hierarchy. Keep both layers through the existing levels.
- In 2022 Standard, list rows show votes/share and the surplus partner; opening Likud shows the source of redistributed votes, candidates and the agreement's seat effect. The independent calculator requires a separate calculate/results step. **Keep immediate, reversible recalculation and explicit vote conservation.**
- Moving Meretz above the threshold changes the allocation and candidate boundary immediately; the explanation makes the discontinuity concrete. Keep the hatched long-tail boundary and neutral change marks. Never replace exact votes with editable integer “mandates” in the primary model.
- The coalition page says its arithmetic is not a political assessment and enumerates minimal winning coalitions. Keep this distinction: a mathematically possible 61 is not a prediction of coalition agreement or an exhaustive statement of government-formation law.
- The locality page distinguishes historical votes from national scenario changes and says envelope votes have no geographic location. Keep this limitation beside the map, not buried in Sources. Search found Jerusalem and rendered locality details at phone width.
- Historical 1949 shows `אין` for threshold rather than projecting 3.25% backwards. It also explains a tie that changes allocation order but not final seats. Preserve this era-specific clarity; none of the short media experiences inspected offers comparable depth.
- Verified period-specific identity, joint-list constituent parties, source links, bilingual operation and dark theme are meaningful existing advantages. This study does not independently prove the “every official result reproduced” positioning: README explicitly qualifies 2026 gazette status, and its strongest detailed verification claim concerns 2022.
