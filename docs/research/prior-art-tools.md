# Exploring a deep model without burying its controls

Research date: 4 November 2026. Decision: how to make the Knesset explorer easier to discover and more flexible without weakening its exact, source-faithful model. Recommendation: retain the board and contextual inspectors; replace capability gates with local disclosure. Keep Simple/Standard/Expert as **density presets**, not prerequisites for actions.

This is a comparative expert inspection, not a user study or a claim that these are objectively the world's best interfaces. “Best” here means useful evidence for short paths from intent to edit, understandable consequences, recoverability, precise control, and mobile continuity. Nine public products were opened at desktop and phone sizes; depth and unsuccessful interactions are disclosed below. No accounts, paywalls or CAPTCHA bypasses were used. No email address was submitted. English Wikipedia was neither used nor cited.

### Priority and acceptance evidence

First implement P2, P5 and P7: they remove known extra steps and explain existing capability without changing the exact engine. Then P3 and P8: maintaining context makes flexibility usable. P6, P9 and P10 improve entry and discoverability; retain P1/P4 as invariants throughout.

Proposed acceptance tasks (I, not tests already passed): from a selected list in Simple, change its agreement without touching a global mode; choose a transfer source without leaving its votes; on a phone see seat and majority consequences while editing; return to a named checkpoint; copy a snapshot and reopen identical model inputs in a fresh browser context. Compare completion, hesitation, wrong assumptions and recovery with the current interface. Use Hebrew-first participants as well as English readers; click counts alone cannot validate comprehension.

### Are Simple/Standard/Expert the right mechanism?

**As defaults for density: yes. As capability gates: no (I, high confidence on extra steps; medium confidence on comprehension).** The owner explicitly wants a truly minimal default and unburied flexibility. Those needs conflict when every deeper action requires a global level change. Calling someone “Expert” is also an unnecessary identity decision when all they want is turnout or a different vote source.

Keep the three choices during an incremental change, but reinterpret them as collapsed sections and visible columns. A local “More options” can override the preset for one inspector. Expert can expand exact quotas, candidates, rule internals and sweep settings by default. Rule changes remain explicitly labelled experimental at every density. A source citation, undo, snapshot sharing, vote source and baseline selection should not require proving expertise.

Do not replace levels with fourteen permanently expanded editors. En-ROADS demonstrates breadth with local depth; Desmos demonstrates parameters created at the point of need. Neither proves that elections need an infinite canvas or a formula language. A later rename to “Compact / Detailed / All controls” merits testing in Hebrew; it is not a prerequisite to fixing the gates. Preserve the product's minimal default and update the product/design contracts deliberately if this recommendation is adopted.

### What already works well

- O/C: selecting lists opens a common inspector from the board; count/share/slider are coherent views of one edit, with a visible proportional-balancing explanation. Reuse this rather than adding another edit form.
- O/C: the neutral change marks, official-result comparison, hatched threshold boundary and 61-seat milestone make political identity and mathematical consequence separable.
- O: Compare goes beyond two pictures: it names indirect changes and candidates entering/leaving. This is unusually valuable model explanation.
- O: merger controls already show retention (100% initially), candidate order and a computed seat-change preview. Joint-list controls explicitly distinguish a post-election faction split (seats unchanged) from running separate lists (assumed vote shares and dropped surplus agreement). These are patterns to extend, not missing features.
- O/C: sourced bloc presets are distinguished from “Your grouping”; bloc instructions explicitly say seats/votes do not change. Constituent parties are visible rather than flattening every joint list.
- O/C: undo, action logs, reversible scenario opening and account-free compressed snapshot links are strong foundations. The successful reopen checked the scenario name; exact input equivalence across all actions was not exhaustively tested here.
- O: Hebrew default, mirrored layout and dark phone board are present. This inspection does not certify all combinations or accessibility.
- C/O: every tool is named even below its level, which is better than removing its existence. The remaining issue is the gate and navigation density, not total invisibility.

## What to avoid

- Global level changes as the price of changing an already visible object; this adds a conceptual step even when the number of clicks is small.
- A command palette as the only entrance. Search accelerates known tasks; it does not teach a newcomer what can be changed.
- Copying canvas gestures indiscriminately: vote counts, eligibility and surplus agreements need visible labels and exact values, not solely dragging or right-clicking.
- Treating “every visible thing is a control” as a reason to make every click mutate the model. A seat/figure should inspect first; a deliberate labelled operation should change votes or structure.
- Mandatory onboarding tours, multi-page questionnaires or a wizard for continuous exploration. Guided stages are appropriate when assumptions must be collected and reviewed, as in a split or import.
- Hiding invalid choices without reasons, or removing a partner's agreement silently. Explain constraints at the point of choice.
- Treating share links as auto-saved cloud documents; presenting browser storage as a backup; implying a public gallery submission is equivalent to copying a private snapshot.
- Copying En-ROADS' phone scaling, LOOPY's clipped dialog or Falstad's narrow circuit viewport. Desktop excellence does not establish mobile quality.
- Judging quality by fewer controls or clicks alone. A one-click merger with unstated retention assumptions is less trustworthy than a short, explicit review.

The nine products are nine independently maintained interface families, not nine independent usability experiments. First-party docs are authoritative about supported workflows, with obvious promotional incentives and possible version drift. This report makes no causal claim that copying a pattern will improve retention. No statistical sample, time-on-task distribution, touch target audit, source word-count metric or screenshot-based score was measured. The primary gap is real Hebrew-speaking newcomer testing; physical touch, accessibility, cross-browser behavior and exhaustive persistence round trips also remain untested.

Deliberate counterevidence pass: looked for hidden controls, account boundaries, excessive setup, mobile clipping and loss of result context in otherwise strong references. Excalidraw's resize camera recovery, tldraw's sign-in sharing boundary, En-ROADS' tiny phone scale, LOOPY's clipping, Falstad's rail, Debt Fixer's overlay and Datawrapper's delayed stage body prevent a blanket endorsement of any product.

Sources are linked at their supporting product entries; these are canonical locators rather than temporary screenshots or search results. Local evidence is the component paths and task/screenshot records above. Jina searches also surfaced secondary videos/community pages; those were discovery leads, not cited evidence. Figma, Flourish, Observable, Sheets Explore and retirement/tax calculators were not hands-on tested here; they are deliberately not presented as reviewed products. The nine chosen products cover policy simulation, creative/engineering inspectors, linked mathematical representations, a budget scenario tool and a staged data editor without requiring login.
