# Owner questions R1: family names, joint-list warning, New Hope

Research as of **7 November 2026**, for the next owner-questions page. Research only; no application or data changes. English renderings of Hebrew labels below are editorial translations unless explicitly identified as an English source quotation. No personal political opinions are reproduced.

**Recommendations:** retain **ימין רדיקלי / Radical right** for now; offer the eight attested alternatives below with their limitations. Warn that a sandbox vote split is an assumption, not a measured election result. Keep **תקווה חדשה / New Hope** as a constituent party of the Likud list: evidence supports a distinct registered electoral participant with one elected candidate, but not a separate 26th-Knesset faction. The 26th Knesset has not opened as of this report.

## A. A usable name for the family

### What is actually being named

The decision criterion is a familiar Israeli Hebrew expression that fits the selected organisations across time, distinguishes them from other right-wing parties, and does not silently replace ideology with a present-day coalition test. “Neutral” means descriptive without an inherent endorsement or condemnation; it does not mean every participant will accept it. Reception assessments and the fit matrix are **editorial judgments**, not measured public-opinion findings.

`src/families/catalogue.ts` loads the definitions; the actual full membership is in `data/meta/families/radical-right.json`. Its union of selected party IDs contains **12** entries:

- התחיה (`tehiya`), צומת (`tzomet`), מולדת (`moledet`), כך (`kach`).
- חרות — התנועה הלאומית (`herut-national-movement`), התקווה (`hatikva`), ארץ ישראל שלנו (`eretz-yisrael-shelanu`).
- תקומה / האיחוד הלאומי–תקומה / הציונות הדתית (`tkuma`), עוצמה יהודית (`otzma-yehudit`), נעם (`noam`).
- ישראל ביתנו (`yisrael-beiteinu`), **only within the 2003 National Union entry** (`k16`, `16-ל`). This is a discrepancy with the brief's description of it as outside the group, not grounds to edit membership during this research.
- `vehicle-21-קי`: the 2019 list called ארץ ישראל שלנו, selected by an **inferred** classification and an observed dataset identity, not a verified organisational identification with the older party of that name.

“National Union” is also an alliance name; it must not be treated as an additional single organisational lineage. A selected constituent does not establish the ideology of every partner in its joint list: notably, the 2026 selected Religious Zionism list also contains Zehut. The preset starts selecting lists in **1973 (k8)**; empty earlier elections must not suggest that these parties existed throughout 1949–2026.

**Strongest classification evidence:** Kenig and Rahat's [*המפלגות בישראל 2021–1992*, chapter 13, printed pp. 595–596](https://www.idi.org.il/media/20997/%D7%94%D7%9E%D7%A4%D7%9C%D7%92%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%91%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%9C-2021-1992.pdf#page=595) explicitly groups Tzomet, Moledet, Herut, Tkuma, Hatikva, Eretz Yisrael Shelanu, Otzma Yehudit and Noam as radical-right parties; its footnote places Kach and Tehiya in the same historical discussion. This is unusually close to the preset, although its historical window is narrower. It does **not** validate the anonymous 2019 vehicle or every later election assignment. Israel Beiteinu receives a separate immigrant-party chapter: the book is not evidence for simply putting all of its history in this family.

## B. Warning when splitting a joint list's votes

### What the sources establish, and what is our inference

**Documented:** parties may submit a joint candidate list with identified candidate affiliations. Election totals attach to that list. **Inference from that ballot structure:** official totals cannot establish how many list voters supported each constituent party; counting elected candidates by affiliation does not identify separate party vote totals. Surveys may estimate preferences but cannot turn the recorded list vote into observed constituent-party votes. The statement “nobody knows” should therefore be expressed precisely as **the election results do not reveal the division**, rather than ruling out all survey evidence.

Useful short source quotation in Hebrew, [Shapira and Rahat, IDI, *ריצה ברשימות משותפות בבחירות: למי זה טוב?*, 27 April 2026](https://www.idi.org.il/articles/63813), section on the legal basis:

> שתי מפלגות או יותר יכולות להגיש לוועדת הבחירות המרכזית רשימת מועמדים משותפת

Our English translation, **not a published English IDI quotation**:

> Two or more parties may submit a joint candidate list to the Central Elections Committee.

A published English quotation, [IDI, *The Joint List*, undated profile](https://en.idi.org.il/israeli-elections-and-parties/parties/the-joint-list/):

> The Joint List was formed shortly before the 2015 elections, combining candidates from three Arab parties as well as from Hadash.

These are quotable structural explanations, **not direct quotations asserting that constituent votes cannot be identified**. Targeted IDI Hebrew/English searches and the inspected passages did not yield that exact warning. Do not attribute our inference or proposed UI sentence to IDI. The Hebrew article also explains that elected representatives initially constitute a common faction and can split by component parties; it does not equate faction splits with recoverable vote splits.

Authoritative corroboration: the [CEC's 2026 Likud candidate-list page](https://www.gov.il/he/pages/halikud-tikvahadasha_iist29) identifies two submitting parties and each candidate's party; the [CEC national-results endpoint](https://votes26.bechirot.gov.il/nationalresults?lang=he) is represented in the repository's `data/raw/k26/official-results.json` as **one** מחל row. Both are inspected through existing repository captures; current direct CEC access returned 403 and was not circumvented. The [Knesset lexicon “רשימה”](https://main.knesset.gov.il/About/Lexicon/pages/list.aspx) was search-discovered but geographically inaccessible on opening, so its snippet is not used as decisive evidence or as a verified quotation.

### Proposed original UI copy

**One-line warning — Hebrew**

> הפיצול הוא הנחה לתרחיש: הקולות נספרו לרשימה המשותפת, ולא ידוע כמה מהם ניתנו לכל מפלגה.

**One-line warning — English**

> This split is a scenario assumption: votes were counted for the joint list, and each party's share is unknown.

**(i) — Hebrew**

> בבחירות מצביעים לרשימה, לא בנפרד למפלגות המרכיבות אותה. אפשר לזהות את שיוכם המפלגתי של הנבחרים, אך לא להסיק ממנו את חלוקת קולות הרשימה. כאן אפשר לבחור חלוקה לצורך תרחיש חלופי; זו אינה תוצאת בחירות או תחזית להתנהגות הבוחרים. ארגז החול מדמה את החשבון שמרכיב את הכנסת, לא את כוונת הבוחרים.

**(i) — English**

> Voters choose a list, not its constituent parties separately. Elected candidates have identifiable party affiliations, but those affiliations do not reveal how the list's votes divide between its parties. Here you can choose a split for an alternative scenario; it is neither an observed election result nor a prediction of voter behaviour. The sandbox models the arithmetic of the Knesset, not voter intention.

This permits the owner's intended experimentation without presenting allocated votes as history. An allocation proportional to seats remains an assumption, including when it is offered as a convenient default.

### Apply the owner's rule

Show **תקווה חדשה / New Hope** as a party component with **one elected seat** under the **27-seat Likud list**. Show the common Likud faction separately when describing parliamentary affiliation; a common faction does not erase a constituent party. In a strict “serving Knesset” timeline before 10 November, label the 26th-Knesset members as **elected / incoming**, not already sworn in. Recheck faction records after opening before asserting the operational faction structure then.

Confidence is **high** in the electoral constituent/seat inference: the candidate filing and list result concern different authoritative records and fit the same election. Confidence is **high** in the presently recorded future faction membership. Confidence is **moderate** in a stronger legal claim of uninterrupted registered existence through the report date: current Registrar export, Gazette and CEC filing point that way, but the export is demonstrably stale in its naming. No inspected cancellation notice or completed formal party-merger record was found. That absence is a research gap, not proof that none exists. A certified Registrar status extract or later Gazette cancellation, plus a House Committee record where relevant, would resolve it.

### Retrieval and reproducibility

Hebrew sources were prioritised. No English Wikipedia page was opened, used or cited; no Hebrew Wikipedia claim was needed. Search results were used for discovery, not promoted over contradictory original documents. Direct HTTP research requests used **`israel-elections-explorer/0.1 (+https://github.com/YiftahCooper/israel-elections)`**. The managed search/web tool does not expose User-Agent control; it was used for discovery and readable public passages, with the decisive fresh HTTP reads made using the specified UA. No observed 429 was retried, no bot challenge solved, and no paywall/login/geographic restriction bypassed.
