# Survey evidence for Israel’s most pressing issues, 1949–2026

Research date: 5 November 2026. Scope: public web sources; no statistical calculation, application code, or Git publication. This is a source-feasibility audit, not a populated historical series. **“Not retrieved” means unresolved, not that the question or data never existed.**

## Finding

**INES is the strongest long-run candidate, but a continuous, comparable, published-topline series was not established.** Relevant questions are verified from 1969 onward, with important changes in wording, response format, coding, and population. IDI supplies occasional useful public toplines; its monthly survey does not ask an identical priority question every month. No qualifying pre-1969 election figure was retrieved. Do not fill those gaps with a historian’s account of the campaign.

Two particularly useful retrieved observations are 1996 INES (a restricted Jewish population) and May 2021 IDI (national adult population, after the election). For 2022, INSS publishes a domestic-priority result, but its exact questionnaire wording/options still need retrieval. For 2026, IDI publishes voting considerations, which must have a different label from national problems.

Eligibility here means an identifiable academic, official-statistics, or established research-institute survey with transparent population, timing, question, and results. Institutional reputation alone does not establish neutrality of an individual question. INES describes itself as independent and nonpartisan; that is its own institutional statement, not an external certification. IDI and INSS have substantive institutional missions; evaluate the instrument and sampling rather than promising value-free research.

## 1. INES / Arian–Shamir / ISDC

**Access:** [INES catalogue](https://socsci4.tau.ac.il/mu2/ines/data/our-data/) is open, with English/Hebrew questionnaires, documentation, SPSS and Stata files. The supplied ines.tau.ac.il address leads to the TAU-hosted project. The catalogue covers each Knesset election from 1969 to 2022, also the 2001 prime-minister election, and now a 2025 between-election study. Listing a dataset does not prove a published issue topline.

**ISDC is a different access route.** Its [FAQ](https://isdc.huji.ac.il/faq) describes access levels, undertakings, source-specific permissions, institutional subscriptions and charges for other users. Do not describe all ISDC data as unrestricted downloads. The TAU catalogue avoids that barrier for the linked INES releases. Public availability is not an unrestricted reuse license.

**Citation:** use the project’s [required format](https://socsci4.tau.ac.il/mu2/ines/how-to-cite/): Israel National Election Studies. 2022. *INES 2022 Election Study Full Release [dataset and documentation]*, project URL. The site separately specifies a 2021 citation for the 2019–2021 release. For earlier waves record investigators, study year, specific phase, document/variable and direct URL; do not invent DOIs or apply the 2022 release title to all years. For ISDC-supplied data additionally acknowledge Israel Social Science Data Center, Hebrew University of Jerusalem, per its FAQ.

The [2022 release appendix](https://socsci4.tau.ac.il/mu2/ines/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2023/06/2022_Appendix-1.pdf) explains pre-/post-election variable prefixes, missing values, mode and weighting variables, and directs readers to the questionnaire appendix for v8 labels. **Comparability judgment:** moderate potential for later single open-response government-problem questions, poor without a published harmonization. Never merge peace/security into one category retrospectively while leaving economy split across inflation/employment/housing. Prompted three choices in 1969, two problems in 1973, and later one open response are different measures. Distinguish raw response text, coding, first mentions, any mentions, sample universe, weights, panel/new sample and pre-/post-election fieldwork.

## 2. IDI Democracy Index, Israeli Voice, and Data Israel

**Democracy Index:** the [official archive](https://en.idi.org.il/centers/1159/1519) and [2024 full English report](https://en.idi.org.il/media/27509/israel-democracy-index-2024-e_for_web_17-2-2025.pdf) verify an annual project since 2003. The report supplies questionnaires, public distributions and selected historical comparisons. It measures democracy, institutions, identity, solidarity and social tensions; **an annual all-topic most-important-problem item was not verified**. “Most acute social tension” is not “most pressing issue.” Do not extrapolate from one report to every year. Access: open online reports; printed-book prices do not imply the linked PDF is paywalled. Citation: named report authors, survey year, exact report title, IDI, page/table, URL; distinguish publication year from survey year.

**Israeli Voice:** [official series page](https://en.idi.org.il/centers/1159/32799) calls it monthly, with current-affairs questions and occasional repeats. Its regular questions concern optimism, not a universal priority ranking. Retrieved archive explicitly includes April 2019 onward; **the proposed “since ~2014” starting date was not substantiated**. Earlier monthly IDI activity must not automatically be assigned this series name. Access: public articles and downloadable question/result PDFs; no login was needed for the examples below.

**Data Israel really does have a searchable question bank.** Verified in [public HTML](https://dataisrael.idi.org.il/) and its linked [site resource/help text](https://dataisrael.idi.org.il/resource.ashx?a=js&b=default_aspx&c=639274750210382750): search survey names, question text or answer options; combine terms; exact phrase; date range; topic and survey-language filters. Results are described as showing question/survey identifiers and fieldwork dates, with questionnaire PDFs, item distributions and Excel/SPSS export actions. Suggested terms: `הבעיה החשובה`, `הבעיה החמורה`, `סדר העדיפויות`, `לטפל`, `תשפיע על הצבעתך`; search early decades separately. A direct search request returned no readable body; **no historical search result set or per-wave frequencies were retrieved**, and export/registration behavior was not tested.

**Critical weighting warning from the portal’s own help:** item-analysis results can be unweighted and differ from published results; some deliberately oversampled surveys suppress the online distributions. A searchable bank does not make every displayed distribution a nationally representative published topline. Prefer the published report, or explicitly approved, reproducible weighted analysis.

**Citation/reuse:** credit authors, monthly edition, actual field dates, Viterbi Family Center / IDI, item/table and direct PDF. The [survey-data terms](https://en.idi.org.il/about/terms-of-use-of-data-from-surveys/) describe personal, one-time research/study permission and prescribe credit: “Data/questionnaires courtesy of Viterbi Family Center for Surveys at the Israel Democracy Institute (RA).” Public access must not be represented as a blanket commercial redistribution license; clarify the intended app reuse if importing underlying survey material.

## 3. Peace Index

[IDI’s archive](https://en.idi.org.il/centers/1159/1520) states it began at TAU’s Tami Steinmetz Center in 1994 and became a joint IDI/TAU project in 2010. IDI labels this archive 2010–2018, but it includes a January 2019 publication: [Elections, Security and the Next Government](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/25582), fielded Jan 2–3, 2019. Thus do not treat the navigation label as a precise endpoint. Complete 1994–2019 inventory and subsequent institutional continuity: **not retrieved**.

The inspected January 2019 release concerns indictments, coalition preferences, Syria and electoral-system reform; **it does not establish a most-important-problem question**. Whole-series absence is not established. Exact qualifying wording, answer categories, historical years with the item and issue toplines: **not retrieved**. The supplied peaceindex.org domain failed resolution; no mirror/proxy was used. IDI archive and Data Israel are legitimate institutional alternatives, not circumvention.

Access: open IDI summaries; original instrument/data access varies and was not exhaustively checked. Citation: authors (e.g. Tamar Hermann/Ephraim Yaar), *Peace Index*, month, TAU/IDI, field dates, original questionnaire/report URL. Comparability: episodic agenda modules need individual inspection; support for peace negotiations or a peace-process index cannot substitute for all-topic issue salience. Peace/Voice results derived from the same IDI survey are one evidence family, not independent corroboration.

## 4. Guttman / historical applied social research / Arian

[Viterbi Center’s official description](https://en.idi.org.il/centers/1159) confirms a public archive inherited from Louis Guttman, spanning over six decades, with more than 1,200 surveys; the center was renamed in 2021. That supports searching the archive, **not an uninterrupted 1947-onward most-important-problem series**. Earliest eligible priority item, original wording, per-year categories and published historical toplines before INES: **not retrieved**.

The INES catalogue explicitly identifies Guttman Institute fieldwork for 1969. This is direct evidence of historical institutional involvement; it is not a second independent survey confirming INES findings. The 1969 population was urban Jewish eligible voters aged 20+, not all Israeli adults.

Asher Arian’s *Security Threatened* and the continuing security/public-opinion surveys are leads. A qualifying table/page with dates, exact security-versus-economy question, answer menu and percentages from that book was **not retrieved**. INSS’s current material does not establish the book’s historical observations. Do not cite the book title as evidence that security was the public’s top concern in a particular election.

Access/citation: use Data Israel’s item identifier, original institute/study title, interviewer population, field date, question and report page; apply portal terms. Book edition/page/ISBN or a citable dataset record still needed for Arian. Comparability presently **unresolved**, especially security-domain forced choices versus unrestricted national problems.

## Retrieved numerical observations (not an automatically approved app dataset)

Every figure here was actually read. Short source excerpts accompany figures; none is reconstructed from party platforms or historical narrative.

**A — 1996 INES, pre-election, restricted population.** Q8–9 in the [findings PDF](https://socsci4.tau.ac.il/mu2/ines/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2023/06/1996_Questionnaire_Eng.pdf) prints **55% security/terror**, 12% peace process, 7% social problems, 3% economy. Evidence quote: “Security and terror in general 55”. Its introduction explicitly explains that right-hand numbers are percentages. Fieldwork May 1–27, N=1,168, representative urban adult Jewish sample **excluding kibbutzim and settlements beyond the Green Line**; interviews at home by Modiin Ezrahi. Display only with this scope, not “55% of Israelis.” The existence of a separate Arab dataset does not change the denominator of this published table.

**B — May 2021 IDI, post-election.** [Q4 PDF](https://en.idi.org.il/media/16382/israeli_voice_index_data_2105_eng-first.pdf), general-public column: national unity **28.9%**, socioeconomic gaps **27.7%**, Palestinian negotiations 10.4%, Iran 8.2%, corruption 8.0%, Arab-population advancement 5.2%, other 6.5%, don’t know 5.2%. Evidence excerpt: “28.9 12.4 32.2 Strengthening the unity of the people”. The PDF orders columns general public / Arabs / Jews. Fieldwork May 30–31; 615 Hebrew and 150 Arabic interviews; adult 18+ representative sample, internet/telephone. Exact question excerpt: “among the following issues which, in your opinion, is the most important”; it is conditional on a new government forming and asks what it should address (complete wording in linked Q4). This is a near tie, not evidence of a decisive national consensus. The [article](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/34615) rounds these two shares to 29% and 28%.

**C — August 2026 IDI, voting considerations.** [Q7 PDF](https://en.idi.org.il/media/32364/august-voice-index-data.pdf) national first-choice shares: economy/cost of living **18.0%**, foreign/security **17.7%**, party head 13.9%, religion/state 12.6%, judicial reform 6.4%. Evidence excerpt: “living 18.0 17.9 18.9”. Q7 wording/options are specified above. Fieldwork Aug 31–Sep 3; 605 Hebrew and 150 Arabic interviews; adult 18+ national sample, internet/telephone. **Do not call economy a clear winner:** the lead is tiny, and a person rather than an issue is an allowed answer. Q7b uses a restricted follow-up denominator. The [article’s](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/66177) first-plus-second-choice summaries therefore must not be substituted for first-choice shares or combined by naive addition. Jewish and Arab menus differ.

**D — December 2022 INSS, domestic priorities after election.** The [2022–2023 report](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/national-security-index-2023/) identifies cost of living **37%** and personal security/governance **36%** as domestic priorities for the incoming government. Evidence quote: “37 percent and 36 percent, respectively”. Methodology: December 2022, N=1,208 Jewish/Arab adults 18+, internet/telephone, Midgam. Original exact priority question and all answer categories **not retrieved**, so hold this figure from the strict production rule pending that check. Do not re-label it the most pressing issue across domestic and foreign-policy concerns.

2001 is intentionally not a k-number: its direct prime-minister election occurred within k15. The open [2025 INES questionnaire](https://socsci4.tau.ac.il/mu2/ines/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2025/07/2025_Questionnaire_Eng.pdf) has Q8, but is a between-election study; its March-2025 title and February-running headers also require checking actual field dates. It must not be renamed a 2026 election survey or assumed within twelve months of that election.

## Recommended display rule

1. Default card: **“Leading concern in [survey month/year]”**, not an unqualified “the issue of the term.” Require an original academic/official/research-institute survey, an inspected question and complete response menu/coding, a public published topline, identifiable population and field dates, and a source link. Prefer fieldwork in the 12 months **before** election day; choose the closest eligible pre-election survey by a declared rule, not the most convenient result.
2. If only post-election evidence within 12 months exists, display a visibly separate **“After the election”** observation with the field date. Never imply it explains the vote. For a government-term card anchor to that government’s formation date; do not reuse evidence across all governments in a Knesset without a dated label.
3. Keep three types separate: national/government problem; priority among a supplied list/domain; issue influencing vote. Independent ratings of many actions, budget tradeoffs, social tensions, values and security-threat rankings cannot silently fill the first type. Under a strictly academic-only interpretation, IDI/INSS need explicit eligibility as research institutes; broadcaster polls are excluded.
4. Show the largest **published category**, percentage, denominator, and close contenders. Call it the largest reported share, not a statistically proven winner. Flag close results descriptively unless a valid design-based comparison is published. Keep don’t-knows visible in the denominator note. No aggregation, recoding or weighted calculations in this research task.
5. Info control: exact Hebrew/English wording and original language; prompted/open; number of choices; answer/coding list; survey producer and pollster; field dates; interview mode; N; population/exclusions; weights; pre/post timing; report/page/question; direct original URL; any coding mismatch. Keep each observation’s source version so changing URLs do not erase provenance.
6. If no qualifying observation exists, show **“No comparable survey figure retrieved”**. A question-only dataset can be a research link but cannot supply an invented percentage. Future weighted analysis, if separately authorized and licensed, must be labeled “analysis of survey microdata,” with variable, weight, missing-value treatment and harmonization retained; it is not a published topline.

## Gaps, challenge findings and handoff

- **Pre-1969:** no qualifying item/topline retrieved. Archival Guttman material means “nothing exists” is not established. These six elections remain empty under the proposed rule.
- **Question versus result:** most INES waves have an instrument but no retrieved topline here. This is a substantive publication gap, not evidence that government-priority questions were absent.
- **Early extraction:** 1973–1984 English PDFs yielded empty text; text codebooks establish item labels/categories, not exact interviewer wording. Original PDFs/OCR need a separately scoped inspection before production.
- **Coding:** recent INES appendices are detailed and differ, including a verified Hebrew/English 2022 mismatch. A common variable number does not establish a common measurement.
- **IDI:** question-search capability verified, but no returned historical result inventory/export validated. Its own help warns about unweighted frequencies and suppressed oversample reports. An archive-wide query is the most promising next research step.
- **INSS:** 2022 domestic result is promising; retrieve the original item and full choices before use. Threat/values/budget questions elsewhere cannot validate its wording.
- **Other sources:** no qualifying Taub, CBS or broadcaster item established; media retrieval partly blocked. No claim of exhaustive absence is warranted.
- **Reuse:** open access and citable excerpts differ from rights to redistribute questionnaires/data. IDI’s specific terms and ISDC’s access rules need to be respected by any later data-import workflow.
