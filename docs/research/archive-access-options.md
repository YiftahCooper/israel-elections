# Getting into the historical archives: options, and how you can help

*Written 7 November 2026; rewritten in plain language on 8 November 2026. Nothing was removed: every fact, date, number and source of the earlier version is kept.*

## In short

- **What this is about.** For elections from 1949 to 1992, the app is missing some historical facts. The main gaps are: which party recommended whom to the President after each election, which parties were in the coalition or supported it from outside, how each member voted when a new government asked for the Knesset’s confidence, and when members changed party groups in the Knesset. This document looks at where those facts are kept and how we can lawfully get at them.
- **Why it exists.** Our automated attempts to read the Knesset, the State Archives and the National Library were often refused. We needed to know which routes are realistic, what they cost, and what is allowed, before spending time or money.
- **What was found.** The most useful quick route is reading old news reports from the Jewish Telegraphic Agency (JTA), a news agency whose archive is free to read online, and checking them against the Israel Democracy Institute (IDI) election summaries. The most valuable records (Knesset session minutes and State Archives files) are reachable, but mostly by a person asking, not by a program downloading.
- **What we will not do.** We will not get around blocks, solve "are you a robot?" checks, use other people’s logins, or buy a subscription before we know exactly what it covers and what we may do with it.
- **Where you come in.** A small, lawful packet of help from you would unlock most of it: opening a few documents in your own browser, trying the National Library’s newspaper viewer for a few dates, asking one library about its holdings, and sending three short enquiry emails (drafts are below, in Hebrew and English).
- **Nothing has been bought, requested or sent.** No account was made and no email was sent.
- **What you need to decide.** Whether to help with these steps, and how much. See question 17, "Trusted sources, and the archives: how you can help", on the [questions page](https://claude.ai/artifact/VNauxmRhqwnzvx8JYsxqCu). Option 17a: go ahead with the first step (our own reading), you send the three emails and ask a library when you can, and you are sent an exact list of a few pages to open in your own browser. Option 17b: only the first step for now. The recommendation is 17a. Nothing has been decided yet.
- **Related.** [Trusted sources: a practical reading plan](trusted-sources.md) lists the books and newspapers to use. The earlier technical report, [archive-access-routes.md](archive-access-routes.md), keeps the earlier connection tests, search settings and document fingerprints; it is unchanged.

## Scope, and what this document is not

The research was done on **7 November 2026**. It covers Hebrew and English evidence for **1949–1992** on:

- **presidential consultations**: after an election, the President meets each party’s representatives, who recommend who should try to form a government;
- **coalition membership**: which parties formally joined the government’s coalition;
- **outside support**: parties that backed the government from outside the coalition;
- **investiture votes**: the Knesset vote that approves a new government (also called the confidence vote);
- **faction changes**: a faction is a party group inside the Knesset; members sometimes split off or move.

This is an assessment of access, **not a finished historical dataset and not a legal opinion**.

**The recommendation in one paragraph.** Start reading specific JTA reports now and check them against the IDI summaries. Ask you for a small packet of primary records from the Knesset and the Israel State Archives (ISA). Ask the National Library of Israel (NLI) to sort out newspaper access, and to approve any bulk download before we build any automation. Do not buy a subscription until we know exactly which years it holds and what this project is allowed to do with it.

## How the evidence was judged

**How each source was seen.** Every source below is marked with how closely it was checked on 7 November 2026:

- **opened and read**: the page was opened with the web research tool and the relevant text was read;
- **seen only in search results**: only the official or institutional search-index text was seen; sometimes opening the page then failed;
- **from earlier project research**: evidence from earlier work in this project, read again locally but not repeated;
- **not checked**: a possible lead that was not examined.

An online calendar of newspaper issues that opens is not the same as a downloaded newspaper page.

**No direct requests to the archives were made in this research.** The web research tool cannot identify itself the way we require, and cannot show exactly how the archive’s server answered. So its results show what is documented, not that our own program could connect. Refusals reported below are what the tool saw. Any future approved automated request must identify itself with the project’s own name and address (see the technical notes), and never with your email address.

**Hard limits.** No solving of "are you a robot?" checks; no getting around logins or paywalls; no proxies or changing internet addresses; no borrowed credentials; no account registrations, payments or messages. If a site refuses, we stop. If a site says "too many requests, try later", we wait as told. English Wikipedia was not opened or used as evidence; results from it that came up in searches were excluded.

**Automated access.** A site may officially offer an interface for programs (an API, an "application programming interface"). That allows a method of access. It does not automatically allow us to copy all the content or reuse it in bulk. Where permission was not clearly given, the recommendation is to read by hand or to ask the provider. Silence is not permission. Access through a university or library must be through your own eligibility, never someone else’s login or a trick with network location.

**What counts as success.** For each disputed fact, a readable page we can cite, with its date, the round of consultations it belongs to, and what we are allowed to keep and do with it. A catalogue entry or a search hit is not enough.

**About the rankings.** The ranking below estimates how much useful evidence we get for the researcher’s effort. It is **not measured coverage, and not a measured response time** from any provider. Sources are grouped by the collection they really come from. For example, Tel Aviv University’s copy of the National Library’s historical newspapers is the same collection, and copies of one syndicated article are one source, not several.

## What kind of record answers each question

| Question | Best record | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Each faction’s recommendation to the President | The minutes or letters of the consultations; clear, dated news reports from the time | Do not work it out from which parties later joined the coalition or how they voted on confidence. Keep the first round after an election apart from later rounds in mid-term and from failed attempts to form a government. 1949 needs its own constitutional context. |
| Coalition membership and outside support | Coalition agreements, the government’s statement to the Knesset, dated support agreements and later changes | Having a minister, being formally in the coalition, supporting from outside, and agreeing to abstain are separate facts. Record the period each one applied. |
| The investiture vote total, and how each member voted | The full Divrei HaKnesset session record (the official printed minutes of the Knesset’s plenary sessions), including the voting pages | A total is not a **roll call** (a record of how each named member voted). Early minutes may give totals without names. Ask whether a named vote was recorded at all, rather than rebuilding one. |
| Faction changes | Knesset notices and committee decisions on factions, with the dates they took effect | The list voters chose, the faction in the Knesset and the party behind it are not always the same thing. A yearbook gives only a dated snapshot. |

### Newspapers from the time, and the National Library

#### Source 1 (S01): [JTA report on the 1969 consultations, 11 March 1969](https://www.jta.org/archive/splinter-groups-tell-president-shazar-of-objections-to-naming-of-golda-meir)

- **What it gives (opened and read; JTA journalism; JTA story of 11 March 1969):** During President Shazar’s consultations, it names the Free Centre’s objection to Golda Meir and HaOlam HaZeh’s preference for Rabin. The page links the original daily bulletin and says the archive covers 1923–2008. This shows clearly that JTA holds exactly the kind of party-by-party evidence we need.
- **Limits:** These consultations followed Prime Minister Eshkol’s death. They are not the round after the election later in 1969, so they must not fill the table of first-round recommendations. An article can leave out parties, contain copying errors, or report what was expected before the final decision. The original bulletin link was not opened.
- **Access, cost and automation:** The article opened publicly, with no login or payment. [JTA’s history page](https://www.jta.org/about-us/jta-history) describes free archive access (seen only in search results; opening the full page was refused). [JTA’s terms of use](https://www.jta.org/terms-of-use) were refused, so no permission for automation or republication was established. Read specific articles and cite them. Ask permission before collecting articles in bulk or sharing full text with AI tools.

#### Source 2 (S02): [JTA report on the 1959 election result](https://www.jta.org/archive/mapai-wins-in-israel-elections-general-zionists-suffer-serious-loss)

- **What it gives (seen only in search results; JTA journalism; a different event from source 1):** Dated 5 November 1959, with a link to the original bulletin. A starting point for the coalition talks that followed. Search the dates after it using Ben-Zvi/Ben Zvi, Ben-Gurion/Ben Gurion, president, consultations, cabinet and the party names.
- **Limits:** Seen only in search results. A report of election results does not show recommendations. The same access limits as source 1 apply. Copies of the same wire report elsewhere are the same evidence, not extra confirmation.

#### Source 3 (S03): [NLI developer services](https://www.nli.org.il/en/research-and-teach/open-library)

The National Library of Israel (NLI) runs the Historical Jewish Press site (often called JPress), which holds scanned Hebrew newspapers.

- **What it gives (opened and read; official documentation; NLI interfaces):** The library advertises a search interface for programs, IIIF image services (a standard way to serve digitised pages and images), and sign-up for an access key. It warns that copyright can limit use. A [documented example of a newspaper search](https://www.nli.org.il/en/research-and-teach/open-library/search-api/user-help) and earlier live results are kept in the earlier report (from earlier project research).
- **Limits:** A newer observation in the task brief takes priority: your existing access key works for basic catalogue searches, but searches for newspapers, and searches in Hebrew, fail with a server error. There is no reason to get another key, and a catalogue search is not the same as reading the article text. We did not confirm any supported bulk-harvesting service, search configuration, free bulk programme, export limit, or a way to get the newspapers’ OCR text (the machine-read text of a scanned page). Harvesting catalogue records would not give page images anyway. IIIF still needs a valid item description and the rights to use it.
- **Access, cost and automation:** Public documentation; your key is already held privately. Current prices and limits are unknown. Ask the NLI for: the supported newspaper search settings, issue and page identifiers, export of OCR text, how to find an item’s IIIF description, and research licensing. [He-Story](https://www.openu.ac.il/he-story/) (seen only in search results) documents a 2017 joint project between the NLI and OMILab. The [OCR pipeline](https://github.com/omilab/2022_OCR_Pipeline) is evidence of that partnership (from earlier project research). It is not an open licence to its newspaper data and not a standing offer of bulk access.

#### Source 4 (S04): [Historical Jewish Press terms of use](https://www.nli.org.il/en/discover/newspapers/jpress/terms-of-use)

### Official records

#### Source 7 (S07): [Israel State Archives portal](https://www.archives.gov.il/)

The Israel State Archives (ISA) holds the government’s official files, including presidential and cabinet records.

- **What it gives (seen in search results, plus earlier project research; official service and earlier catalogue evidence; ISA):** The home page, as indexed, tells users to contact staff and says publications are moving from the old site. The indexed phone number is **02-5680680**; visits need an appointment; confirm before travelling.

  - **נ-571/2**: formation of the 15th government, 31 December 1973 to 10 March 1974, described as including the consultations;
  - **נ-56/1**: resignation of the 9th government and formation of the 10th, 16 December 1959 to 2 November 1961. This is not automatically the first round of December 1959; ask staff to find that round separately.

  A label saying a file is open is not a scan. Permission to use the catalogue details does not cover the documents themselves.
- **Published editions and "documents of the month" (earlier leads; current scans not read):** [Navon and the tenth Knesset election](https://catalog.archives.gov.il/site/chapter/navon-and-10-knesset-election/) covers the 1981 consultations (see the existing [1977–2006 report](pm-recommendations-1977-2006.md)). - **Terms, cost and automation (seen only in search results):** The indexed text of the [portal’s terms of use (PDF)](https://www.archives.gov.il/media/anzaw4uw/תנאי-שימוש-באתר-ארכיון-המדינה.pdf) separates reasonable use of individual or small numbers of documents from copying a whole database or a large subject collection. Ask for PDF or TIFF files, OCR text if they have it, which pages are missing or closed, how to apply to open closed material, the copy cost, reuse rights, and whether we may process copies on our own computer or with AI tools. Register personally only if staff or the current terms require it. Confirm whether research can be done remotely or needs a reading-room appointment; a visitor-centre tour is not research access. No request or order was made.
- **Flickr and YouTube:** This short search found no official ISA photo or video channel with relevant original consultation material. Ask the ISA for its official links. Search results for the US National Archives (`archives.gov`, with no `.il`) and the IDF archive (`archives.mod.gov.il`) are different institutions. Photos and clips are useful background but are unlikely to list every party’s recommendation. Being on a platform gives no general right to download or reuse.

## How you can help: a small, lawful packet of evidence

1. **Your own browser first (30–60 minutes).** Open the known Knesset extracts and the ISA publications and file references (sources 7 and 8). Do not change your location or try to beat a check. If normal access is refused, stop and use the provider’s contact route. If saving is offered and allowed, save a selected PDF or a few screenshots that show the date, page and identifier. This is your own lawful access. It is not a request to solve a "robot check" for an agent or to hand over your browser’s login data.
2. **A newspaper trial (30–60 minutes).** In the NLI’s normal viewer, try Davar for **8–9 December 1959** and the days of consultations around them, and Maariv for the same dates.
