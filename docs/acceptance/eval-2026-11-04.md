# Independent demo evaluation — 4 November 2026

The explorer does **not meet the demo bar**. It is a substantial, working simulator with fast loading, useful drawings, reversible vote edits and functioning scenario exchange, but it is not yet a trustworthy, independently explorable demo. A party-level bloc edit produces contradictory totals on different pages; running a joint list separately produces misleading change figures; phone controls lose their names or their displayed state. These are reasons to stop a demo, not polish requests. The distance to the bar is principally integration and interaction quality, rather than missing breadth: **3 blockers, 5 majors and 2 minors** are recorded below. Full provenance and accessibility certification remain unestablished; passing the existing tests does not establish either.

## Findings

### E1 — blocker — Compare silently ignores party-level bloc changes

**Combinations:** all eight, directly reproduced.

**Steps:** choose 2022 → Standard → Edit blocs → choose **New Hope (4 seats)** beneath National Unity → Move here in Coalition → close editor → Compare, against official result.

**Observed:** Overview and What changed say Coalition **68**, Opposition **52**. Compare says Coalition **64 → 64**, Opposition **56 → 56** and draws unchanged bloc bars. The same scenario contradicts itself. **Expected:** Compare must represent the four transferred members, even though list votes and total seats did not change.

**Evidence:** `.backup-scratch/eval/shots/en-light-desktop-E1-overview.png` and `en-light-desktop-E1-compare.png`; all eight equivalents exist with the language-theme-device prefix. **Likely pointer/inference:** `src/app/components/Compare.tsx`, `blocSums`, sums `list.bloc` and does not use constituent-party seat assignments. Observed counts are independent of that diagnosis.

### E2 — blocker — Coalitions' bloc shortcut loads the wrong membership

**Combinations:** all eight, directly reproduced.

**Steps:** make E1's move → Coalitions → Start from **Coalition**.

**Observed:** the shortcut constructs **64**, despite the edited Coalition being **68**. New Hope cannot be selected independently in this list-only builder. **Expected:** a control bearing the edited bloc's name must load that bloc, or clearly state that party-level groupings cannot be represented. A visitor cannot infer which of the two totals to trust.

**Evidence:** `.backup-scratch/eval/shots/en-light-desktop-E2-coalition.png`, paired with E1 Overview; all eight variants captured. **Likely pointer/inference:** `src/app/components/SeatSecurity.tsx`, `Coalitions` / `startFrom`, uses whole-list membership. This is a distinct broken task sharing E1's integration boundary.

### E3 — blocker — Each separated party inherits the entire joint list's baseline

**Combinations:** all eight in the main matrix.

**Steps:** 2022 → change Likud to 1,300,000 votes → Standard → United Torah Judaism → What if they had run separately? → Run as 2 separate lists → close details and inspect the two new list rows.

**Observed:** both resulting parties receive zero seats in this edited scenario; **each** says seven fewer than the official result. The original joint list had seven seats in total, not seven per component. With an otherwise untouched 2022 baseline, Agudat Yisrael instead wins four and is labelled down three; the comparison is still against the entire seven-seat parent. **Expected:** a clearly stated, nonduplicating baseline or an explicit “not directly comparable” treatment. The displayed per-party losses imply a false total loss.

**Evidence:** `.backup-scratch/eval/shots/en-light-phone-T3-merge-split.png` (both rows in the full-page capture); untouched-baseline example `en-light-desktop-E7-split-baseline.png`. **Likely pointer/inference:** `src/app/components/ListBars.tsx`, `baseSeats`, sums parent `originIds` independently for each child. Model allocation can be correct while this explanation is false.

### E4 — major — Seat safety promises exact minima without preserving the search qualification

**Combinations:** exactness wording inspected in both languages/themes/devices; source boundary applies throughout.

**Steps:** Standard → Seat safety → open “How this is computed”.

**Observed:** the tooltip says each figure is the exact minimum, obtained vote by vote, and that the numbers are exact. DEVELOPMENT.md explicitly warns that large seat searches can be sampled and this view drops exactness. Source inspection confirms `securityPart` reduces a search result to a number/null; `SecurityRow` has no exactness/reason field. **Expected:** distinguish a proven minimum from a sampled result or an unsuccessful bounded search.

**Evidence:** `.backup-scratch/eval/shots/E4-exact-tooltip.png`; matrix-independent `*-E4-exact.png` captures the view in all eight combinations. **Likely pointer:** `src/app/analysisTasks.ts` (`securityPart`, `SecurityRow`), `src/app/components/SeatSecurity.tsx`.

**Proof limit:** this review did not establish that a specific displayed 2022 number is numerically wrong or capture its engine `exact:false` result. The finding is the unsupported universal guarantee and lost qualification, not a claimed counterexample to a particular minimum. Severity is major rather than counting an unproved numerical error as a blocker.

### E5 — major — Simple phone view conceals which election is open

**Combinations:** all four phone combinations. Desktop selector measured 224 px; phone Simple selector measured **39 px English / 41 px Hebrew**.

**Steps:** fresh phone view → remain in Simple → choose 2022 → close the native picker.

**Observed:** the election control shrinks to approximately an arrow-width white box; its selected election text is invisible. The first-time visitor looking at the results cannot see their year. **Expected:** the current election remains visibly identified without reopening its picker or changing detail level.

**Evidence:** `.backup-scratch/eval/shots/en-light-phone-T1-result.png`, `en-light-phone-E5-header.png`, `he-light-phone-E5-header.png`; dark equivalents also captured. **Likely pointer/inference:** `src/app/styles.css`, phone `.band select.election { width: auto; flex: 1 }`, competing with the level controls and tools; `TopBar.tsx`.

### E6 — major — Phone Save/share and Start over have no accessible name

**Combinations:** all four phones; desktop text provides the names.

**Steps:** phone → inspect or navigate the header controls → find Save/share; edit any votes to enable Start over.

**Observed:** labels are `display:none`; these two buttons have neither aria-label nor title. The menu button is present and works when its icon is clicked, but role/name discovery of “Save & share” fails. **Expected:** the same named controls remain identifiable to assistive technology and unambiguous when represented by icons.

**Evidence:** `.backup-scratch/eval/shots/en-light-phone-E5-header.png`; `.backup-scratch/eval/craft.json` records both empty visible text and absent accessible-name attributes. **Likely pointer:** `src/app/components/TopBar.tsx`, corresponding buttons; `.band .tool .label` phone CSS. This is not a claim that pointer saving fails.

### E7 — major — Clickable result rows are missing from the keyboard interface

**Combinations:** all eight DOM inspections; keyboard navigation sampled English desktop and Hebrew phone.

**Steps:** Standard → Compare → expand “Every list…” → Tab through the table → attempt to open Likud's details using Enter; repeat on Seat safety's list rows.

**Observed:** Compare rows respond to pointer clicks but are plain `tr` with tabindex **−1**, no interactive descendant and no keyboard activation. Seat safety likewise puts click handlers on unfocusable rows. Tab can reach the scrollable table, not its list controls. **Expected:** the list-detail action available to a pointer is also reachable and named from the keyboard.

**Evidence:** `.backup-scratch/eval/shots/en-light-desktop-E3-keyboard-rows.png`; all eight equivalents, with row attributes recorded in `focused.json`. **Likely pointer:** `src/app/components/Compare.tsx` table rows, `src/app/components/SeatSecurity.tsx` `.sec-row`. The 2 px visible focus ring on reachable tabs does not solve missing controls.

### E8 — major — Splitting a list leaks its raw identifier into locality results

**Combinations:** all eight after the main split scenario.

**Steps:** complete E3 → Expert → Localities → search “tel aviv” → select Tel Aviv–Yafo (5000) → inspect the selected locality table.

**Observed:** the historical United Torah Judaism row is now labelled **`25-ג`**, with 2,204 votes, instead of its name. The map's warning correctly explains historical votes and estimated split colours, but does not explain this identifier. **Expected:** historical rows retain the original list's readable identity, with the scenario mapping explained separately.

**Evidence:** `.backup-scratch/eval/shots/en-light-phone-T8-places.png`, all eight `*-T8-places.png` captures. **Likely pointer/inference:** `src/app/components/LocalityMap.tsx`, selected-results table resolves historical IDs only against current `s.lists` and falls back to raw IDs when the parent has disappeared.

### E9 — minor — Escape does not close details while a field has focus

**Combinations:** directly reproduced in all eight combinations.

**Steps:** open Likud → focus Votes → press Escape without making a change.

**Observed:** details remain open; repeated Escape while focus remains in the input still leaves the sheet open. **Expected:** Escape should eventually close what was opened, as the bar specifies. The visible Close button works, so this is a recoverable interruption rather than a dead end.

**Evidence:** `.backup-scratch/eval/shots/en-light-phone-E6-escape.png`; all eight variants and `escapeStillOpen: 1` in `craft.json`. **Likely pointer/inference:** `src/app/App.tsx` returns early for INPUT/TEXTAREA/SELECT before its global Escape handler. Field editing can justify the first Escape reverting text; it does not explain an unchanged field permanently swallowing the close path.

### E10 — minor — Compare uses party colours to draw change, against the declared design rule

**Combinations:** all eight changed scenario comparisons.

**Steps:** change Likud votes → Compare → inspect Seats gained and lost.

**Observed:** gain/loss bars use party colour; the design's Neutral Ink Rule explicitly calls for change to be drawn in ink, with party colour reserved for identity. The numbers and signed direction remain understandable, so this is a design consistency defect, not a claim of partisan manipulation.

**Expected:** the same distinction between identity and change across pages. **Evidence:** `.backup-scratch/eval/shots/en-light-desktop-T5-compare.png`, `he-dark-phone-T5-compare.png`; saved-comparison captures also show it. **Likely pointer:** `src/app/components/Compare.tsx`, `.cmp-neg` / `.cmp-pos` inline `background: colorOfGroup(g)`.

## Integration results and limits

The main sequence exercised vote edits, merger preview/application, an after-election faction split, a separate electoral run, bloc rename/recolour/add, whole-list tap movement and constituent-party tap movement, a winning coalition, official comparison, scenario naming/save/share/reopening/export/import, allocation explanation and locality selection. Undo/Redo were exercised after vote changes, merging, both split types, recolouring and membership moves. The list seat totals and chamber remained usable; What changed reflected the principal allocation edits. Saved comparison was separately exercised using a distinct saved baseline and a duplicated edited scenario, rather than mistaking the intentionally excluded current save for a missing comparison option.

A supplementary drag/delete/election-round-trip probe completed on English light desktop and touch phone. It showed a resulting membership change and its persistence, but the attempted drop did **not** establish that the intended item landed in the intended new bloc; no blanket drag pass is claimed. Tap movement is the better-established path. Bloc deletion and undo/redo controls worked in that probe. The review did not exhaust all edits in Turnout, Sensitivity, Rules or Expert identity/candidate editors; therefore “every tool” history correctness stays U. Nor did it independently prove all exported fields or candidate identities survived every round trip: the shared scenarios reopened with the same rendered list seat figures, and import/export completed through the UI.

Keyboard-only tab/arrow navigation sampled the task pages in English desktop and Hebrew phone, with visible 2 px blue focus outlines on reachable tabs, plus Escape/menu checks. The locality level-switch keyboard sequence did not complete reliably in the probe, and the entire eight-task editing sequence was **not** completed using only the keyboard. E6/E7 already prevent certifying the requested every-control condition. Native file-dialog operation, a screen reader, 200% zoom, high-contrast mode and full contrast measurement were not tested. Axe-core was absent from installed dependencies, so no axe scan was run and no dependencies were added. Colour/non-colour accessibility remains unestablished, not implicitly passed.

Historical spot checks opened k8, k15 and k26 in each combination and confirmed the chamber, labels and lack of document horizontal overflow; they were not full historical repetitions of all editing workflows. Provenance was sampled through the UI and supplied repository documentation, not independently reconciled for every datum or live external link. Candidate transliteration gaps, historical roster limitations, national-vote/locality modelling limits and pending 2026 publication were visibly or documentarily disclosed; that is good practice, but does not erase E4. The owner's final 15-minute acceptance play was not performed by this reviewer.
